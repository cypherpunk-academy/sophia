# Chapter 28

Simeon sat cross-legged on a hill and looked down on a lake. He was wearing a white linen dress and was deeply absorbed in meditation. His face radiated an infinite calm.

The three shepherds arrived at the place where they had left their flock and the two dogs jumped joyfully towards them. One jumped up to Amon and licked his face. Amon took a step backwards and said in a firm tone, "No! Back to your place!" The dog pressed down on the ground and looked at him. "Good," Amon called out, "Come!" He gestured for him to jump back on. The dog immediately came back up and licked his face again. They played with each other. The herd was still complete, all the supplies still there.
"Do you think we can keep them?" asked Amon Nahor while hugging the dog.
Nahor looked at him with a grin, "Do you think you could get rid of him again, even if you wanted to?" The dog barked loudly and they both laughed.

A few weeks later, Sophia, Joseph and the child arrived in Nazareth. They were followed by about 200 people with ponies, donkeys, wagons and many things in their luggage. The town looked sleepy, but as the crowd poured into the village, many came out and were amazed. What was going on here? Why so many people? The priest went out to meet them and was startled! Behind him ran a cluster of people who stopped as he stopped and looked in horror at Sophia and the child, "Sophia! Is this your child?" Sophia dismounted and walked towards him with the child. His lips trembled, "Stop! I must not come near you."
Sophia smiled at him, "My name is Maria. And you are right, you must not come near me. But I am allowed to approach you." She stopped in front of him, smiled warmly at him and showed him the child. He looked at her fearfully, then at the child, who shone through all his limbs. He was confused. He felt free and light. He wondered what was happening here and Sophia answered him, "Here is your Lord. He has arrived." The priest was overwhelmed and sank to his knees. He stammered softly, "You are my Lord." Then he put his forehead on the ground and tears dripped from his face into the sand. Sophia put a hand on his shoulder and said softly, "He's just telling me that you should solemnly slaughter a lamb on your altar." Then the group moved on.
Mary had also come out of the house and called for Anna. She saw the crowd and did not know who they were or where they came from. Then she saw the donkey with Sophia and the child. Her heart began to beat like a ship's drum. She ran, passing between the people until she stood in front of the donkey and opened her mouth wide. She wanted to say something, but nothing came out. Sophia got off the donkey and went to her. Without saying anything, she gave her the child in her arms. Mary took it, pressed it gently to her heart and closed her eyes. She saw the whole host of angels that had led the shepherds to the stable appear above her. Her chest swelled, she almost couldn't hold the energy, and at one point she said, "Yes, I will. I will give my life for this." She handed the child back to Sophia and went down on her knees. Sophia followed her and gently laid her forehead against Maria's. They knelt like this for a while. Their mother stood behind them and watched with teary eyes.

On the hill, Simeon's body lay on its back, looking up to heaven with empty eyes, but still with deep contentment.
