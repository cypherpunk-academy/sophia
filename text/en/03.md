# Chapter 3

Zechariah looked into the bronze mirror in his bedroom and examined the finely woven undergarment that was always given to the chief priest of his ward. He wore it for the first time that day. It was a light brownish white and had many embroideries with patterns and religious symbols. At chest level, an embroidery showed the biblical scene of Eve in paradise showing Adam a fruit.
"That suits you! It suits you well, doesn't it?" said his wife Elizabeth, who was standing behind him and also looking in the mirror. Zechariah grinned and nodded. "You will stand in it someday in Jerusalem in the great temple and prepare the sacrifice --- and give the sacrificial speech to the huge crowd." "Yes, six years from now. That's when it's our turn, our department. But I'll wear something about it then." "Of course, the high priest's robe, which is kept there in the inner temple. Shall I get you your robe for here?" "Yes. Thank you!"
A distant shout made him look towards the window from where a slight breeze was blowing into the room. "I must hurry," he thought. On a hill opposite the village, Tomas picked up a stone and threw it with all his might at a wild dog running away. His brother was kneeling over a lamb that was bleeding profusely. "It will die, Tomas! Run to the priest!" "I'm running to Zach." "Don't say Zach anymore! You can't say Zach anymore." Tomas grabbed a handful of earth and stuffed it into a small bag. Then he ran off, over the little garden wall, down the path to the village green, he cut across a front garden and took the path up to Zachariah's house.
Elizabeth held out the heavy priestly robe to Zacharias and he held out his arms to her. It fitted exactly, even the length had been adjusted to his height. "Hurry up, I have to go out in a minute" said Zacharias, while Elisabeth tied the cords of the robe together at the back. She nodded. "One more thing --- good, ready! Let me look at you ..." But Zacharias had already disappeared towards the front door. Entering the front garden, Tomas reached the outside gate. He was about to knock when Zacharias called from inside, "Come in, Tomas! It's open." Tomas was a little startled, he hadn't seen Zacharias, but quickly composed himself and went through the gate. He reached for the hand Zacharias held out to him, got down on his knees and kissed the priest's ring. Zacharias helped him up and asked, "What do you need?" "The lamb -- a wild dog -- we need to slaughter. It's dying." "I can't go with you now. But if you had me some earth from your place ..." Tomas held up the little bag and smiled at Zacharias. He took it, got down on his knees and reached into it with one hand. Then he pulled the bag to his chest, mumbled a few words and gave it back to Tomas, who ran off with it. Zacharias looked after him, shaking his head.
"Zach!" said Elisabeth behind him, "there is someone else here who needs you." Zachariah turned and saw a young slender man of about 18. He had shining but somewhat tired eyes. "Aaron! What's wrong with you?" "What do you think is wrong with him?" interjected Elizabeth, smiling, "haven't you noticed anything wrong with Mary lately?" "I haven't seen her." He closed his eyes. "She carries a child under her heart, doesn't she?" "Not any more!" Elizabeth looked at Aaron. Aaron said excitedly, "We have a son. Two hours ago." Zechariah lifted his head to heaven, took a slow breath and after a while said, "Welcome!" He looked at Aaron, "Your first child! Your first son. When do we want to circumcise him?" Aaron was a little confused about the question and said, "The day after tomorrow. In my family, everyone is circumcised on the third day." "Good! The day after tomorrow then. You have everything you need for that?" Aaron nodded, "Yes." He smiled, swallowed, looked at Zacharias and couldn't stop himself. He burst into a loud, free laugh, laughing and laughing. Zacharias laughed with him and so did Elizabeth. After a while Zacharias hugged Aaron and he went out of the garden gate with tears of joy in his eyes. Zacharias looked after him. He shook his head with a smile and turned to Elisabeth: "It is always so beautiful when joy breaks its way ..."
But Elisabeth was no longer standing. She had turned away and sunk to her knees. She held her chest with both hands. Tears were streaming from her eyes. Zacharias knelt down in front of her: "You were laughing with us just now. What happened? Are you in pain?" After a while she said softly, "I was so happy for Aaron. His laugh. He took an inner leap, to man, to father, and then laughed so freely in front of you. It's the greatest thing for him, his first son --- and then ---" she squinted her eyes, "and then -- I thought of my own son, our son." Zacharias embraced her and rested his head on her shoulder. "Elizabeth, that was almost 40 years ago ...... and we never had a child ...... you never carried a child under your heart." "But it was there! I talked to him. An angel came and told me I was going to have him. Angels can't lie. -- I talked to him! He told me that he was on his way to earth, I just needed a little patience." She cried harder. "But 40 years is such a long time ..." Zechariah pressed her against him, "Elizabeth --- you cannot have children. We can't have children. We know that now. And that's fine, that's the way God wants it." "Did you hear that from an angel, or do you just think that?" "You're well over 50 years old. Women don't have children at that age."
At that moment, Mary came jumping from behind and hugged them both. "Uncle! Auntie!" she exclaimed with joy. She faltered, "OHHHH! Auntie, you're crying! What's the matter? Did you fall down?" Anna and Sophia joined them. Zacharias looked at them, "This is a surprise! Welcome!" He stood up. Anna came up to him and hugged him. "We all wanted to see the newly crowned priest superior, from the little village of Cana in Galilee." Elizabeth wiped the tears from her eyes, hugged Mary and stood up too. For a time all five of them stood together on the grass in front of the house, holding each other. No one said a word. Until suddenly Mary jumped away and called out, "Where is Joseph's wooden horse?" She disappeared into the house. Sophia ran after her.
"I met Simeon," Anna said, "he's fine. He walked the three days alone from Jerusalem to Nazareth again." "About Sophia," Zacharias commented, "-- he sees something special in the girl. Has he said anything more about what that is now, except that she is a chosen one?" "No, not very clearly." "Not very clearly?" asked Elizabeth, looking at her urgently. "Nothing really, he said something about what she's going to experience, but I'm sure that's not why he's been visiting ever since she was born." "Do you want to say what it was?" asked Zacharias. Anna looked down at the floor. "He said that she will experience the same thing that I experienced with her." Zacharias and Elisabeth looked at each other, startled. Elisabeth grabbed Anna's shoulder, "It can't be." Anna nodded but said nothing. "And he stayed calm through it?" "He reassured me and meant that everything would be all right with it." Elisabeth clutched her chest, "Did he say that as a man or as a priest or ..." "... as someone who sees what is going to happen." Elisabeth shook her head, "He's human. He can be wrong." Zechariah interrupted her, "Simeon is wrong about something he is quite sure about? I find that hard to imagine." He looked calmly at Elizabeth. "I have to go to the temple now, I hope to see you afterwards Anna, I want to hear more about this." He gave her a long hug and walked out the gate. They both watched him for a while.

Anna turned to Elizabeth again, "Do you remember how I came here then, in the middle of the night, with Sophia in my womb, and you took me in? -- And Zacharias hesitated at first, because Klophas had been dead for over a year?" Elizabeth nodded. "Yes, and I thought before that we had a curse in the family: I had been childless for 30 years and you for 20 ... And here you came, with a child. I was beside myself with joy." "And I couldn't understand it at all! How? How? How could I have a child? And Zacharias didn't understand it either. I understood him in it completely. And you argued with him!" "Yes, that one time. That's when I argued with him!" "And he ran out into the temple, full of rage, and met Simeon there, right at the entrance, just like that, a foreign priest from Jerusalem, how often does that happen? And he put his hands on his shoulders and said that my child was purely conceived and that he should take as truth everything I would say -- and he said that before Zacharias even told him about me." "Simeon told me that he then just looked at him for a long time. And then he left again without saying a word." "And he came back to us and begged my forgiveness on his knees, and then I was allowed to stay with you."
She smiled and closed her eyes. "And he helped me so much with the birth, prepared everything so beautifully and solemnly, there was always a fire in the room, and fine smoke." "Yes, he did everything he could and knew." "And then all of a sudden there she was, Sophia, and she changed my life. Born without pain. A magic child. I was so happy at the time. Everything was light and bright." "Yes, we were all happy! She was like my daughter. She is like my daughter. And because of me it could have always stayed that way, but Zacharias thought about what would happen if you left the house together and were seen. But then, yes, Joachim came! Out of nowhere."

Anna sat down on the floor and Elisabeth followed her. She closed her eyes and after a while a tear ran down her eye. Elisabeth smiled, "Joachim. He came like a Roman soldier, with sure steps, his breastplate shining." "He is a Roman soldier, a Jewish Roman soldier." "And what else was the first thing he said to you?" Anna, sitting down, assumed the stature of a soldier and spoke in a dark voice: "Anna of Nazareth! Why do you hide from me?" "He had been looking for you for a year. No one in the city knew where you had gone." "And then he took me and bought the house in Nazareth and we moved in. Three of us. Just a regular family... And soon there were four of us." "And no one asked you any questions about Sophia. Not one, her whole life." "Not a single one. I was just there again. He'd taken me away, then brought me back." "Sometimes complicated knots just unravel." They embraced.

Anna and the two children stayed for a few days and then headed back. Elizabeth and Zacharias looked at the three of them for a long time as they said goodbye. Then they went into the house. Zacharias turned to Elizabeth: "When was Joachim actually in Nazareth for the first time?" "You mean when he was wounded and Anna nursed him? -- That was two years before he came here." Zacharias nodded. "Mhmh." "Why do you ask?" "It's not true that she never slept in his room before he came back." "She didn't say that. She just said she'd never been with him."

Just before sunset, Anna and the children reached their house in Nazareth. Anna grasped the door beam and said a few words and then they went in together.
