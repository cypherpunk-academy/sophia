# Chapter 16

Sophia left the path to the rose garden and ran up a meadow and from there into an olive grove. She just kept running, she didn't know the direction, but something told her that she wasn't lost, that she would come out where she wanted to go. She felt completely safe, even though she was alone. After about two hours, she came across a path she knew, the one that led to her aunt's home in Cana. She smiled.
Around noon she reached Elizabeth's house and knocked joyfully on the gate: "Elizabeth! Open up!" she called from outside over the wall and she heard Eilsabeth's footsteps. The gate opened and she was about to fall around her neck, when Elisabeth put her hand over her heart and looked at Sophia with a surprised expression on her face. Sophia wanted to say something, but Elisabeth made a hand signal that she should wait. The child in her belly moved visibly. She took a step back and said aloud in a slightly altered voice: "I welcome my Lord! The one who has come to loosen the world from its spell, to put together the pieces into which it has shattered, to meet each man in the depths of his selfishness so that he can learn who he is and who the other is. And you Sophia, you blessing, happy, chosen among all women, you bear the fruit within you that will soon see the light of day as King of Israel, Lord of our destiny and path to freedom. To him we will prepare a way."
Elizabeth and Sophia looked at each other for a long time without saying a word. Then Sophia suddenly laughed and jumped around Elisabeth's neck. She detached herself again and ran through the garden shouting, "I am his servant! And always will be. I will lead him into life! He who has come to set right what has become crooked in humanity, what has lost its hold, or balance, the connection with the whole, with everything, with the earth and the heavens. He will bring the heavens to earth and wear the earth as his garment. He will bring love where there is now mere wisdom, create empty spaces where there is compulsion and indifference and selfishness," she faltered and sank to her knees, "His destiny will drive a stake through my heart..." She held her hands before her eyes and crouched on the floor.
Elizabeth came and put her hand on her back, "He will conquer death."
Sophia lay down with her back on the grass and looked up at the sky, "He will conquer death."
Elizabeth dropped down beside her and so they looked up at the sky together for most of the afternoon.
When Zacharias came home, they had dinner together and then watched a beautiful spring sunset.

Joseph crouched in front of the altar wall in his temple cottage. Gabriel sat larger than life on a protruding shelf in the wall. He gazed at Joseph for some time. Then he said in a powerful voice that made Joseph sit up, "Joseph of Bethlehem!" Joseph looked at him with tired eyes and was about to crouch down on the floor again, then Gabriel said, "Joseph, one of the great heroes of human history." "I ... am ... not a ... Hero," Joseph replied quietly. "I am no match for life. When it comes down to it, when the big things happen, I fail."
Gabriel laughed so loudly that Joseph felt it in his bones: "No hero is a match for life at the beginning. Heroes only become great through their failures."
Joseph: "I am not at the beginning of my life. At the beginning of my life was my son and he was killed, along with his mother, and I believed that I had overcome all that." Gabriel looked at him, "In the beginning of your life there was no son of yours. She was not pregnant by you." Joseph froze. After a while he pointed to where the figure of the boy had lain in the altar wall and wanted to ask, "He is not my son?" but the figure had disappeared.
Gabriel: "Your only son Sophia carries under her heart. He is the King of Israel, he will take the burden of being alone from the world, he will envelop the Messiah who will arrive on earth in 30 years."
Joseph softly, "The Messiah." He closed his eyes. "The Messiah ... But --- but what am I to do, weak as I am, where I make a big mistake at the moment when everything matters. When the Messiah comes ..."
Gariel interrupted him, "You humans come to earth to make mistakes! That is part of your task. You can't make mistakes in the heavens. Only here on earth. I cannot make mistakes. That is why I am not fit to do what you are going to do. And --- small people make small mistakes, and are well served by them; big people make big mistakes, and are equally well served by them. Own your mistake, learn what you can from it. Learn, learn, learn, take it as a gift and I tell you that you don't even know how big your mistake really was. You believed you had a son you never had, and all that has come of it for you is now before you."
Joseph looked deep into his eyes, "I must return to Bethlehem, hence your greeting earlier."
Gabriel nodded, "But not yet. Because --- you will not go alone."
Joseph looked at him, but Gabriel had already disappeared.
