# Chapter 13

On the morning of the day they were to go to Joseph, Sophia got up an hour early. She fetched fresh water from the well and hay from a small shed and took both to the animal enclosures. She took the little bunny that Josef had had on his hand and stroked it for a while. She wondered if she should take it with her, but decided not to. Then she went into the kitchen, lit the cooker and put a pot of water on it.
Her mother looked into the kitchen a little sleepily, "You're up early!"
Sophia: "Yes. I couldn't sleep any more. I'm looking forward to the trip."
Anna: "It's the first time you've left town in a long time ..."
Sophia: "The first time since Lidia was killed."
Anna nodded, "Yes --- that's not what I was going to say."
An hour later the three of them were on their way. Maria trotted along behind them a little listlessly, occasionally kicking a stone down the street or against a house wall. Anna and Sophia paid no attention to her, it was clear that Anna was not full of joy to make such a long hike to a place where she knew no one. But she couldn't stay at home alone either.
"I'll quickly go to the market stalls and buy a ham for Joseph," Anna said and turned into the street that led to the market square. Sophia looked questioningly at Maria and gestured with her hand for them to follow Mother together, but Maria looked in a different direction, pursed her lips, and then pointed to a spot further up the road.
"What?" asked Sophia, "What do you mean?" Then she saw what Maria meant. She froze. There stood Thomas, half with his back to them, with his two older brothers. Sophia stormed off. She stopped only when she was almost behind him and hit him on the shoulder, so that she still caught his neck a little. Thomas turned and looked in amazement into two fierce eyes. Sophia stared at him and then pushed him, who was half a head taller, away from her with force. He landed in the arms of one of his brothers.
Sophia shouted, "She's dead! Dead! Because you were too cowardly."
Thomas looked confused.
Sophia: "She didn't want to go with you. You tricked her." She shoved him again as hard as she could. Thomas stumbled backwards and fell with his bottom on a stone. He groaned, jumped up, grabbed Sophia and yanked her to the ground. There he held her tightly.
Sophia spat at him, "You can't control yourself. See now?" She tried to free herself. The older of the brothers grabbed Thomas by his shirt collar, yanked him up and punched him in the face with force. Thomas fell to the floor and stayed there. He held his cheek. Sophia stood up and looked at the brother with an evil glare, "He is still your brother. Shame on you." She turned and walked back to Maria with angry steps. The other brother clenched a fist and wanted to follow her, but the older one held him back.
"What was that?" asked Anna, who had just come back and had seen Sophia from a distance with the group of boys. Maria shrugged her shoulders.
Sophia: "Oh, just Thomas. He's an asshole. I pushed him and he pushed me to the ground and then held me down."
Anna: "WHAT, he held you down?" She turned to the group of boys and took a few steps in their direction until they recognised her and ran away. Anna exhaled, "Let's let them go for today. They're a bad bunch. Let's go."
After a long walk, with a pause in the middle for Sophia and Mary to bathe in a pond, they turned into the valley where Joseph had his farm.
Joseph waited in the square in front of the farm and watched as the small group came up the rose-covered path. At the top, he smiled at Anna and shook her hand. Then he held out his hand to Maria, who took it a little sceptically and gave it a quick squeeze. He turned to Sophia, but instead of taking his hand, she came closer and hugged him. Anna and Mary looked on in amazement.
"Welcome!" said Joseph then to everyone. The ox will be here in an hour. I'll be happy to show you my farm first, if you like. They all nodded. And so they began a round of the farmyard, first the stable, where a donkey stuck his head out of the window. As the four approached him, he brayed in the pumping way donkeys bray, only a lot louder than normal. Maria and Sophia covered their ears.
"He's happy to see you!" said Joseph. "He seems to like you already."
Sophia walked up to him and tried to put her hand on his muzzle, but he snapped at her hand.
"She has nothing to eat, Anton," Josef said from behind. The donkey tilted his head and turned it to the side. Sophia stroked his neck with her hand. After a while, the donkey turned his head and held out the other side of his neck to her. Everyone laughed. Sophia came a little closer and hugged his head, which the donkey calmly accepted.
Joseph nodded: "You like each other ..."
Sophia: "I want a donkey too."
Joseph: "It looks like you have one now."
As the group stood in front of the carpentry workshop, the door opened and Klophas came out. He smiled at the group and froze. He just stopped in his tracks and said nothing.
Josef: "This is Klophas, my first apprentice in 20 years. Klophas, this is Anna, Sophia and Maria."
Klophas couldn't get a word out. Anna stepped up to him, looked at him powerfully and held out her hand. He looked back, took it and smiled.
"Klophas, show us your horse!" said Josef and Klophas nodded.
"He has a horse?" asked Sophia in amazement.
Klophas nodded and went to the workbench that stood in front of the carpenter's shop. He reached under it, took out a rocking horse and proudly placed it on the workbench.
"For children up to six years old," he said in a matter-of-fact tone.
Sophia laughed, "A rocking horse." She went to the table and stroked it with her hands. "It's very beautiful! We have one like it at my Aunt Elisabeth's too. Maria come, look!"
Maria looked at Sophia and then came. She stroked her hand over the horse once, "Yes, it's nice, nice and smooth."
Klophas looked at her but said nothing. Joseph grinned. He walked over to him, put his hand on his shoulder and said softly in his ear, "Well, that's an impressive young woman, isn't it?"
Klophas looked at him in amazement, then turned to him and straightened up. He said in a normal tone, "I lost myself for a moment. I think I have myself again." Then he turned and walked towards Sophia, extended his hand to her and said, "I haven't said hello to you yet. Hello!" He smiled warmly at her. She smiled back and took his hand. Then he turned to Maria and greeted her too. Anna shook her head slightly and then looked at Joseph. He smiled, shrugged his shoulders and said softly, "Klophas ..."
After an hour, a farmer came up the path to the farm with the new ox. Sophia and Maria ran to meet him. Sophia ran straight towards the ox, he was big and very strong. The farmer stopped him. She stood directly in front of him and embraced his huge head.
"Careful!" said the farmer, "this is not a bull, but he can get angry."
Sophia pressed her cheek to his head and said, "Welcome!" The ox remained calm. In the meantime, Maria had gathered a bunch of herbs and leaves and held them out to the ox. He took it with his long tongue and began to chew. She also stroked his head and said in his ear, "May I ride up on you?" The ox did not respond. Maria called out, "Thank you!" Ran around the farmer and indicated that she wanted to be lifted up. The farmer hesitated, but then did so. So the three of them came up the stile to the farm with the ox. Maria was laughing all the time, she was obviously comfortable up on the ox.
Clophas looked spellbound at Mary, who was enthroned high up on the ox, spreading her arms now and then and looking up at the sky. Joseph said to him quietly: "These are not normal girls. You can see that well, can't you?" He smiled. Klophas did not answer.
Mary jumped down from the ox right in front of Klophas and laughed. Sophia walked up to Joseph, "What are we going to do with him now?"
Joseph: "First we give him a thorough wash. A welcome wash. Do you want to do that?"
Sophia shouted, "Yes!"
Mary: "Me too!"
Joseph: "Then I'll get two brushes. Klophas can you fetch a big bucket of water from the well?"
Klophas: "I will. And a brush for me too."
The three of them had a lot of fun scrubbing the ox, and he obviously did too, he didn't make a sound and let everything wash over him. Anna and Josef sat on a bench and looked down into the valley. Sometimes they looked at the three of them and were happy. They began to splash each other too.
Josef: "It's amazing that Klophas is so involved. He is usually very correct and precise in everything. He very rarely goes out of his way. And I haven't seen him like that before."
Anna: "Yes, when the girls play, nothing stays dry. Not even with Simeon. You know him, don't you?"
Josef smiled: "I know him very well."
Anna: "Sophia sometimes just jumps on his lap at full run and hugs him."
Josef: "Yes, he won't experience that anywhere else." They both laughed.
Josef pointed towards the horizon, "See that straight line of dark clouds back there?"
Anna nodded.
Josef: "That's a storm. It will probably be here in about two hours."
Anna: "Do you think so --- we still wanted to go to Elisabeth and Zacharias in the village. Then maybe we'll wait out the storm."
Joseph stood up and looked towards the horizon, "I don't see the end. I can't tell how long it will last, maybe it will be dark. If you still want to meet them in the light, you'll have to leave soon, I think."
Anna: "That looks a bit threatening. But can you tell for sure?"
Josef: "The clouds have a language of their own. I'm not quite in it yet, but I already know a few things."
Anna: "Yes, then we'll let the three of them wash the ox and then leave. We can come back tomorrow morning, I feel we are not done with our meeting yet."
Josef: "Yes, with pleasure! Any time. I will prepare a meal for you together with Klophas."
Anna smiled.
Then she got up and went to the three of them, who were by now quite wet, "I see you are almost done with the ox, aren't you?"
Maria: "Soon, soon, the feet still, but I am disturbed here all the time by Sophia." She dipped her brush into the water and flung it towards Sophia with the brush.
Sophia laughed and said to Anna, "We'll be done soon ... if Anna would finally finish her feet instead of always splashing water." She dipped her brush into the water and pretended to fling it towards Maria, but then flung it towards Klophas.
He cried out, "Sophia! You can't do that like that. I haven't done anything to you." At that moment a shower from Maria came over to him and he took cover, "Help! I'm being attacked. Attacked!"
Anna laughed. "Okay, we'll be leaving soon. See that you have the ox in the stable by then and that he has something to eat too."
Anna and Klophas called out, "Okay."
Sophia jumped towards her mother, "No, we're not going yet. I still want to ..."
Anna interrupted her and pointed in the direction of the dark clouds, "Joseph thinks we're getting a storm. And we promised Elisabeth and Zacharias that we would come today. And we can't spend the night here either."
Sophia: "Please, I haven't really looked at the temple house yet."
Anna: "Yes, we have been inside."
Sophia: "But I have so many questions. I still want to ask them. And we've been to Eli and Zach's many times."
Anna: "You can ask tomorrow. We'll be back tomorrow. Josef will cook us something."
Sophia looked sad.
Josef stood next to them, "A thunderstorm is a good time to talk in the temple cottage. There's a lot of energy in the room when there's lightning and thunder outside. I'm sure Sophia would like that. I'd be happy to bring her to you after the storm."
Sophia smiled and nodded at him.
Anna: "Are you sure you want to stay here alone?"
Sophia: "Well, I'm not alone. Joseph is here and so is my guardian angel and the other angels."
Anna closed her eyes. She inwardly asked her guardian angel what she should do. Then she opened her eyes and said, "Yes, that's what we'll do."
As the ox stood in the stable next to the donkey, chewing on his hay, Anna and Maria packed their travelling bags and were about to set off. Klophas held out his hand to Maria, who took it.
Klophas: "I don't want you to go. It was so much fun."
Anna: "We'll come back tomorrow."
Maria: "But you can come with us too. Eli and Zach don't mind, I know that. In fact, they like surprise visitors very much. Zach told me that once. There is always magic in such surprises. That's what he said!"
Anna: "No, I don't think that fits now."
Maria: "Yes, you do. You're just scared. I can see that. Of what, actually?"
Anna closed her eyes. After a while she said, "Good. Klophas, let's go. Maria is right, they will be very happy."
Two hours later, the thunderstorm passed directly over the farm.
Lightning struck on the other side of the valley, immediately followed by a loud clap of thunder. Sophia and Joseph sat on seat cushions in the temple cottage and Joseph answered question after question. After a particularly powerful thunderclap, they were both silent for a while. Then Sophia said, "This is a wonderful energy right here. Now it's time to talk to Lilith, isn't it?" She smiled.
Joseph: "Now to speak to Lilith?" He laughed. "I haven't done that yet during a thunderstorm. We can try it." He stood up, picked up a third cushion and placed it so they would be sitting in a triangle. He took a picture of Lilith from a compartment on the altar, looked at it and then put it back. Then he took a lit candle and was about to put it down when he felt a slight breeze in the room. He put the candle next to the picture and turned around. On the cushion sat a beautiful, slightly smiling woman in a beautiful silk dress.
Sophia marvelled at her with wide eyes: 'I can see her! With my normal eyes. I can see you, Lilith. How does that work?"
Lilith: "No, you don't see me with your normal eyes. This is just a special place here. In front of the altar I can appear much more alive to you than in your bedroom. But I don't have a body. You create the image of me all by yourself --- but I'll help you do it as best I can." She looked at Josef with twinkling eyes, "What do you want to see or do?"
Joseph closed his eyes and took a deep breath in and out.
Sophia, "I want to see what it does to Josef when he looks at you."
Joseph smiled, "Yes, that is interesting. I believe you."
Lilith: "Joseph! Look." Lilith lay back on her back, relaxed, bent one leg, stretched one arm back and then stretched her whole body so that her back arched a little but she still remained relaxed. Joseph looked. Sophia was amazed with wide eyes.
Lilith sat back on her cushion as she had at the beginning and looked at Sophia: "Well, did you see?"
Sophia: "Yes! Amazing. That's some beautiful, pure, powerful energy. I haven't seen it that strong before."
Lilith nodded, "I've been practicing for this for a long time ..."
Joseph: "You haven't."
Lilith: "Not practiced like you humans. That's true. But in the beginning I couldn't do very much. It was only over the millennia that I was able to gather more and more of it together --- everything that you humans have forbidden or forgotten or changed around these powers, that I was able to pick up and make mine."
Sophia: "You are what we humans have lost?"
Lilith: "Yes, but only in my small territory. Every demon has its territory where it picks up what treasure humanity leaves lying around."
Sophia: "And what is ... your territory?"
Lilith eyed Sophia for a long moment, then said, "You're very young to ask such a question."
"But I can ask it!" replied Sophia, wondering a little where she got that answer.
Lilith: "My field is the connection of wisdom and imagination. Later, humans will one day call that joining communion, union on a higher plane, but it's a long time before then."
Sophia looked in silence for a long while. Thunder was still rumbling outside and from time to time the glow of lightning could be seen. Joseph looked at her in amazement and said nothing either.
Sophia: "But why do we humans lose such a beautiful thing? Why do we give it up?"
Lilith: "It is very easy to lose oneself in this power, to lose oneself in it, much like Clophas lost himself earlier when he saw Mary. It was just too much for him. He suddenly wanted something for himself. And he got stuck and didn't know what he could do in such a situation and so he froze. Such a powerful energy as you have just seen between me and Joseph can make men want to possess the woman from whom it emanates, even sometimes do violence to her, just to get closer to the energy --- not Joseph, he is not susceptible to it --- but some even want to have me, and that is quite stupid. I am a demoness, no human can have me or oppress me or force me to do anything."
Sophia listened with her mouth slightly open. She marvelled and thought, "I'm talking to a demoness who tells me things I've never heard before and yet meets no obstacle in my mind." Then she also felt a force restraining her that seemed to emanate from her mother, that wanted her not to get close to Lilith, not to listen to her, to be wary of her, and what did Joseph have that he was not vulnerable to temptations from a demoness who did just that so well. Why did she now suddenly think it was a lure, she hadn't perceived it that way before. She looked at Joseph and then at Lilith. They both looked at her and smiled warmly. A demoness smiling warmly ... her friend had died because she had met her --- Sophia was getting tired, so many different thoughts was in her head and heart --- she could not sort it out. She just said, "I'm getting so tired, I need to lie down for a bit." And moments later, she lay with her head on the pillow and slept.
Joseph got up, got a light blanket and covered Sophia with it. Then he sat back on his pillow and looked at Lilith, "Was that you?"
Lilith shook her head, "I wake up, I don't let people fall asleep. Others do that. She's just confused, she couldn't get it all together. One turn too many and she wouldn't let go of anything. Then you humans often fall asleep. It's enough for Zacharias to give a sermon. No sooner does he start than half the audience is asleep."
Joseph nodded, and yawned. "I'm getting tired too. Why is that?"
Lilith laughed at him, "You mean you understand everything I said."
Joseph: "What don't I understand?"
Lilith: "You don't understand why you feel you want me, even though I am a demoness..."
But Joseph already didn't hear the answer. He lay with his head on his pillow, sleeping as soundly as Sophia.
Lilith had disappeared.
