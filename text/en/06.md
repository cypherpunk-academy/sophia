# Chapter 6

Sophia still held her head to the door and counted backwards, "3 ... 2 ... 1!" She opened the door carefully. Her little sister came from behind and tried to hold her, "Hey, Sophia, no! Mother told you to stay here." Sophia turned and looked at her sister, "I can't! I have to see what's there. She was screaming so strangely." "You can't go! Mother was quite serious." "I have to," Sophia cried, tearing herself away and running out into the street. Her mother had gone to the left, following her. "That way is Clara's house. That's where they went!" thought Sophia. And sure enough, as she turned into the path leading to the house, she saw a crowd of people there. She was taken aback. So many people and most of them seemed strange to her. "Where did these people come from?" she wondered, "why so many and what are they doing in front of Clara's house?" Her mother was standing with Clara at the front door in front of a young man who was blocking their way. Sophia pressed herself against a wall so that her mother would not see her looking up the path. Her mother held out her index finger to the man and said loudly and forcefully, "YOU LET ME IN THERE!" She moved her face very close to the man's face, who visibly got smaller. Then she pushed him aside and went through the door.
Sophia became more agitated. "What's happening? Why wouldn't Mother and Clara go into the house?" She no longer saw them, she only saw the crowd pressing closer to the entrance. Sophia spotted an apple tree across the street, opposite the house. She took off running down the street, grabbed one of the lower branches and swung up. Then she climbed up the trunk and found a spot where she could push aside the leaves of the tree and thus see into the main room of the house. She saw a group of priests and priest's assistants standing in a row. All with their typical black robes, a cap on their heads and very serious expressions. She saw her mother come in the door, saw her looking at the priest and saying something, but couldn't hear it or otherwise make out exactly what was going on.

Anna stood in the room, looking to the left where the priest stood with his three assistants, all looking dark and serious. Clara sank to her knees at the door and began to pray. Anna looked sternly into the priest's eyes, "What's going on here?" The looked back grimly, but otherwise did not move. She glanced at the bed. Lidia was lying there huddled up, holding her hands over her head. Anna went to the bed and sat on the edge. She put a hand on Lidia's back. "Lidia! Lidia, what's wrong?" "Go away, please," she said softly. "Lidia, tell me what happened." Lidia remained silent. Anna turned to the priest, "What's wrong with her?" The priest pointed almost imperceptibly with his nose to Lidia's stomach. Anna closed her eyes and breathed a few times. "Lidia ..." "I am guilty," Lidia said softly, "I am guilty." Anna gently put her hand on her back. "No, Lidia, you're not ..." "Yes I am, I went with you. Into the mountains. I wasn't allowed to go there alone with him. But I went with him. He pushed me so hard ... I didn't want to ..." "You didn't scream?" "No," she whimpered softly. Anna hugged her. "You will have to marry him. He must not disown you." The priest said from behind her, "He doesn't want her." Anna: "He must." "No. She is engaged." Anna's face turned red. She turned to Lidia, "Lidia, you are ... engaged?" Lidia nodded and began to cry, "I didn't know. My mother ..." Anna turned to the door, where Clara put her hands in front of her face.

Sophia tried to catch any of what was going on in the room from the apple tree but could not. Her tension increased, the crowd grew louder and more excited, pushing even more towards the house. She saw that some had heavy bags with them. "What's in there?" she thought. Some had a stone in their hands. "Why?" She did not understand.

Anna stood up and turned to the priest, "We will find a man for her." The priest shook his head, "It's too late. It was already too late when this happened on the mountain. She should have screamed or run away. The law is quite clear in that case. Evil must be eradicated from our midst." The assistant beside him nodded in agreement. Anna walked up to the priest and looked at him urgently, "Yes! ... By the *entire* congregation. I am, too. And I don't do that. Moses also says thou shalt not kill!" "Except according to the will of God." "Thou shalt not kill is one of our basic laws." "If the law commands, then we kill." "Clophas would not have let you get away with that! Imagine if he were here in this room right now, in front of you. Would you say that now?" "Klophas isn't here anymore." "Yes, he is," she thought, "he lives in people's hearts. That's why so many from other villages were out, because not enough would have come from this village." She looked at the priest for a long time. "Look at Lidia: Is she the evil you want to eradicate? An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth. What has she done that you should ..." He shook his head, "The law is clear!" He crossed his arms. Anna grew louder, "If Klophas were here now, he would read you the riot act." "He's not here anymore." "He would find a solution. He used to say that it's not the people who are evil, it's their actions --- she didn't even know she was engaged, she didn't know the man would do violence to her." "The law is clear!" "And who is the man? Who did this?" The priest took a deep breath, "Thomas, from the goat farmer." "Thomas? He's sixteen, the same age as Lidia. And what happens to him?" "He pays the fifty pieces of silver, he didn't know she was already engaged."

Sophia grew uneasy in the tree. The crowd was getting louder and more excited. A farmer was making his way through the crowd with his son. "Thomas," she thought, "what's he doing there?" His father looked even more scowling than usual and pushed Thomas by the shoulder in front of him. He looked down at the ground in shame. She didn't like him much, but then again, he wasn't as wild and unpredictable as his older brothers who roamed the mountains at night, fighting with the boys from the other villages. He was rather quiet. Her mother had often scolded his brothers for breaking something or chased them from some garden or backyard. She saw Thomas walking towards the door of the house with his father and being let in. "Why?" she asked aloud without anyone hearing, "what are they doing there now?"

"Where is Thomas?" asked Anna of the priest, "I want to speak to him!" "He'll be here in a minute. His father is coming to get him. He thinks he needs to look at the whole thing." Anna got a twinge in her stomach and held her stomach. "That can't be. I don't believe this is happening right now," she thought. Noise could be heard coming from the entrance. The goat farmer pushed his son into the room so that he almost fell over Clara. Anna came towards him threateningly. "WHY DID YOU ...?" Thomas looked at her briefly, rolled his eyes and threw up in front of her. He sank to his knees. Anna grabbed him by the forehead with one hand, "Why did you do that?" "Forgive me! Please. I didn't mean it." He closed his eyes and sank down. Anna took a few steps back and sat on the bed. "We can break the engagement," she said quietly, but with much less force than before. "It's too late," the priest said, "and you of all people ..." He looked at her urgently. "... You of all people should be really careful what you do in this case here -- especially in a case like this." "Pre...cautious?" asked Anna. "Yes. How old is Sophia exactly? And when did you marry Joachim? He never told me exactly. I remember he even dodged me on the question." Anna looked at him in confusion. The priest made a sign to his assistants that they should take Lidia. Anna stood up, wanting to stop them, but one of the assistants got in her way and held her down. Another tied Lidia's hands together with a rope. Then they took her out of the room.

Sophia could no longer stand it on the tree. She climbed down and jumped from the lowest branch to the ground. Then she ran towards the crowd. At that moment she saw the priest's assistants pulling the bound Lidia out of the door. "NO!" screamed Sophia, but no one noticed her because everything was running wildly and shouting and pushing. "NO - NO - NO!" She felt fear, her chest was thumping, it was harder to breathe. The crowd took in the aides with Lidia and followed them to the village square. Sophia couldn't make out what was going on in the square, she followed the crowd, trying to spot what she could. She saw a post in the middle of the square that had not been there before. She kept walking, almost dazed, suddenly a hand hit her shoulder and held her tight. A dark male voice said, "No womenfolk!" A man yanked her around and stood in her way. Sophia instinctively looked left and right and saw that some men had formed a chain there to make sure that only those who were allowed by the rules could go to the square. She was old enough, but a girl.

Anna sat dazed on the bed. An angel appeared behind her. She could feel him clearly. "Anna! You have to go now. Anna!" he said in a warm, soft voice that filled the whole room. She woke up. She shook her head and looked at the priest's assistant who was still standing in front of her. She closed her eyes and said, "I triggered your father twice from the Romans." Then she opened her eyes and slapped him lightly on the cheek with her hand. She pushed him aside and ran out of the house, following the crowd, to the centre of the square where the post stood to which Lidia had just been tied. A man stood in her way, "No women!" She replied loudly, "I am the wife of Klophas. I am allowed to go there! I have been there many times and you know it!" He stepped aside and Anna ran to the front, pushed her way to the front row and saw Lidia there, huddled together, trembling all over, her head pressed against the post. Anna looked wildly around her at what she might be able to do now -- anything -- she called inwardly for her angel, but he remained silent, almost as if she could hear the silence --. Someone shouted, "Stone her!" and then she heard a girl's voice scream a bloodcurdling, "NO!" she looked to her left and froze.

A few moments before, Sophia saw a boy walk out of the circle next to her, away from the crowd. She turned away from the man who had stopped her and walked over to him. "Hello, Levi!" She walked up beside him. "Why are you walking away?" "I don't want to see this. My father wanted me to see this, but I don't want to." "Give me your robe!" He was wearing a grey goatherd's cloak. "You're crazy, why? Why should I give you my frock?" "I'll give it back to you tomorrow." "No, I'm not giving you my frock." Sophia pulled him behind a wall of the house. "Please!" He looked at her in confusion. She smiled, "I'll give you a kiss for it!" He looked at her in amazement. "Really?" "Yes!" "No - really?" "Yessss." "Good." He moved his head closer. "First the robe!" He took it off and handed it to Sophia, who immediately put it on. He came closer again and Sophia grabbed him by the head with both hands, turned him, gave him a kiss on the cheek and ran off. He called after her, "That wasn't a kiss!" "Yes it was, that was a real kiss." She ran towards the village square.
Her heart was beating up to her throat. She got in line behind a man who was also heading for the square and tried not to look any guard in the eye. She succeeded. She got through the chain and pushed her way forward through the crowd, on and on, until she was behind the first row and saw Lidia shaking and full of fear. Sophia was frightened. She could see fear differently from other people. Sometimes it was gruesome to look at, a chaotic thicket of metal spines, but like Lidia's now, she hadn't seen that before. She felt as if Lidia's fear was taking hold of her and freezing her. Then she looked into her eyes and at that moment a tremendous rage rose up inside her, she could hardly hold it in, the anger pressed up her throat. A man across from her shouted, "Stone her!" and then she couldn't hold the rage any longer, she let it out all her strength, "NO!" she shouted as loud as she could and ran into the circle, towards Lidia. "SOPHIA!!!" her mother screamed and ran towards her through the circle. She caught Sophia just before Lidia did, grabbed her as tightly as she could and carried her out of the circle, through the crowd that was giving them space. "Away! Just away!" she thought, nothing else. Sophia kicked and tried to free herself. "Let go of me! Lidia! She's terribly frightened. We have to help her ..." She saw over her mother's shoulder as a stone flew straight at Lidia's eye, spraying blood in all directions. Sophia let out a long scream. Her mother looked around briefly, saw Lidia slump down bloody from the stones and pressed Sophia's face to her chest. So they ran on. Sophia whimpered: "Why Lidia? Why? Why this?" "She was with a man." "No! She couldn't have been. I know her. And so do you." "She was!" "You're lying. It can't be. Not Lidia. You know her." Anna fell silent. "Not Lidia! You know her." "She carries a child under her heart." "That can't be at all. Lidia doesn't do things like that," Sophia cried in exasperation. "Thomas made her." "Tho..." Sophia froze. Her mother squeezed her tighter and walked with her towards her house. Sophia let out another loud long cry, and then another, and another, and another. Her mother squinted her eyes, tears streaming down her face. She held Sophie tightly in her arms and she continued to scream.
