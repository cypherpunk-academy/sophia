# Chapter 24

It was just beginning to get dark when Joseph saw the first house of Bethlehem. He turned joyfully to Sophia and was startled: "Sophia! ... Umm... Mary! What's the matter?" He stopped the animals. Sofia held on to the backs of the two animals with the last of her strength. Her lips were blue, her face white, her eyes almost closed. She fell into Joseph's arms. He sat down with her. The wind swept between the animals. Sophia was trembling all over. Joseph held her for a few minutes.
Josef: "It's not far now. We are almost there." Sophia did not respond. Josef pressed her closer to him. A tear ran down his cheek. "So... Mary! Up! Come!" Sophia lifted her head a tiny bit. She nodded at him and tried to smile. But she couldn't.
Joseph took off his coat and put it around Sophia. Then he stood up, walked to the ox and whispered in its ear, "I know you're at the end of your tether too, but can you take it again?" He tried to talk to the ox the way Sophia always did and the ox didn't move, which was a good sign. He helped Sophia onto his back. She lay down completely on it and nestled against its neck. In this way they moved on. In the village, Joseph quickly found the inn he was looking for. It was at the other end of the town. He opened the garden gate, went to the front door and knocked loudly. Inside he could see light and thought he heard the crackling of a fire. The landlord opened the door and looked at Joseph in amazement.
Host: "Are you ...?"
Joseph: "Yes! --- Hello brother!" He opened his arms. The host did not react. Joseph pulled his arms back. They looked into each other's eyes.
Host: "I am not your brother. Your brother ..."
Joseph: "I know! We used to be like brothers. Do you remember?"
Host: "Maybe. --- Does your brother know you are here."
Joseph looked at him.
Host: "Hmm. Of course not. If he finds out I'm putting you up and doesn't tell him, then ..."
Josef: "Just for one night. We need to warm up and tomorrow we'll move on. No one will know."
The innkeeper thought for a moment and then said, "My house is full. I don't have anything free. I just got another family in. There is no room."
Joseph: "Please! My wife is pregnant."
The innkeeper looked at him in horror, "WHAT, and then you want to come to me? Do you know what your brother will do to me if I help you with this. He pushed Josef outside and slammed the door.
Joseph closed his eyes for a moment and then turned to Sophia.
She was kneeling between the animals and praying. She didn't look any worse than before, she had even gained a little more life. Joseph knelt in front of her.
Sophia said softly, "He won't take us, will he?"
Joseph: "No. My brother knows I'm coming. He threatened him."
They embraced.
Sophia: "There is a way. I know it." But she didn't sound as light as usual when she said things like that.
Joseph: "There is an inn over whose owner my brother has no power. The innkeeper is rough and very forbidding to everyone, but he has a good heart."
Sophia: "Let's go there."
They leaned on the animals together again and walked like that to the centre of town. It had become quite dark by now. Joseph stood in front of the second innkeeper's house and shouted as loud as he could, "LEVI! Come out. There are people in need here, they need a roof for tonight."
A window opened, "WHO CALLS?"
Joseph answered a little less loudly, "Joseph ... Of Nazareth."
Second host, "Don't know him. Get out!" He closed the window.
Sophia had got up and come to Joseph. She clung to him, "Joseph, I think the child is coming soon. It cannot be born here."
Josef put his hand on her head and called out as loud as he could, "LEVI! We know each other. We just need a bed for one night."
With a loud crash, the door slammed open. The innkeeper stood there with a pitchfork in his hand, "MAKE YOU COME FORWARD, YOU HUNGER! I have nothing for you but this." He raised the pitchfork in the air.
Sophia saw the pitchfork and slumped down. The landlord went into the house and slammed the door with a loud crash. Joseph winced. He looked at Sophia, holding her in his arms. She was weak and almost couldn't look out of her eyes. Then she opened her eyes a little, looked at Josef and said, "I'm sorry, Josef, I can't go on ..." Then she fainted.
He bent over her, embraced her and closed his eyes. A chill ran through him. He could not control his body for a few moments. Then he gently took her in his arms and whispered, "Come, we must go on. We have to find a place for the child." He gently placed Sophia on the ox again and took the animals by the collar. He pulled on it, but the donkey stopped.
Joseph to the donkey, "Come on, we must go on. We have to find a place." He pulled again. But the donkey bucked. Another chill went through his body. He sank to his knees.
A hand tapped him on the shoulder from behind. He turned his head and looked behind him.
Third innkeeper: 'Come on, I've got a stable with some hay in it. Just over there. You can stay there until tomorrow."
Joseph took his hand, stood up, "Thank you, brother!" He lifted Sophia trembling from the ox and carried her to the stable. The innkeeper brought the two animals. Inside, Josef prepared a bed of hay between the animals and laid Sophia on it. He thanked the innkeeper again and he closed the gate behind them.
Joseph lay down next to Sophia, put his cloak over them both, took her in his arms and fell asleep too.

Amon pulled his foot close to him and held it with both hands. He sat on a small lambskin. Across from him sat Gallum and Nahir. "It's getting dark! And it's bloody cold. Maybe this is already the coldest day."
Gallum: "Makes no difference to us whether the Messiah is born today or not. There's nothing for us to do. Besides, then your priest also said that he will not come for thirty years."
Nahor: "That is the body that the Messiah gets, that is born now, the human being, what he gets from the earth. The Messiah is not a man. He is a god."
Gallum grumbled, "Whatever." He yawned.
Amon looked at Nahor in surprise, "Now where did that come from?"
Nahor smiled at him and shrugged. Then he yawned too.
Amon: "Brothers, we have a night ahead of us. And you are tired already? What is wrong with you?" He looked at the two. They turned their backs against each other, leaned against each other and fell asleep. Amon yawned too, "Why do I get so tired? I have to stay awake when the others are asleep." Then he turned his back to them too, tried to keep his eyes open and shortly afterwards fell asleep too. All three of them seemed to have moving dreams, sliding back and forth in their sleep, turning their heads, twitching and talking loudly.
Suddenly all three woke up together. Gallum jumped up, slipping on a small patch of ice that had formed on a stone and fell down.
Gallum: "Ouch! Devil! That's cold. The ground is frozen." He held his arm. "And to think I just had such an incredible dream. Unbelievable."
Amon: "Me too. I had an incredible dream too. Like I was in there. But tell me, what did you see?"
Gallum: "Saw and heard: a huge angel. Right in front of us. And he was talking and talking. Like thunder, a voice like that. And then many, many angels all over the sky. A ..., a ..., a field of angels. And they were humming or something." He tried to imitate the sound, but quickly gave up. "Not like that. Different. And the big angel really had a voice like thunder."
Amon: "What did he say?"
Gallum looked at him with wide eyes, "I don't remember. Wait --- the Messiah child ... he's freezing and he's in a stable in Betelehem, we have to help him! Now!"
Amon: "Let's go! He told me it's thirsty and can't drink at its mother's. We need a jug of sheep's milk."
Gallum slapped Nahor on the shoulder, "And what about you? What have you heard?"
Nahor: "That the Messiah child is born in a stable in Bethehem and we will find him if we follow the angels. In the sky."
Everyone looked up and saw nothing. They looked for a while, but the sky was black and empty.
They looked at each other.
"Never mind," said Amon, "we'll find it. "First we'll go down to Bethlehem." He jumped up and the other two followed. At that moment the sky shone with the light of countless angels, like a moving cloud they hovered over the three shepherds. She was beautiful. With eyes and mouths wide open, the shepherds marvelled towards the cloud. After a time, Gallum slapped Amon's arm, "We have to go!"
Amon: "Wait! I still need the milk." He went to the sheep and reached into the box where there was another jug of sheep's milk. Gallum came and picked out the nicest and softest one among the sheepskins they had. He stuffed it under his cloak, right on his belly, "This will keep it nice and warm until we get there."
Nahor called out, "And bring the money bag."
Amon: "The whole thing?"
Nahor: "Of course! You don't know how much they need."
Amon took the jar and money bag and the three set off. Suddenly he stopped, "And who will look after the sheep? We can't just leave the flock alone."
At that moment they heard a nearby bark. A tall, strong, slender dog came slowly towards them and lay down on the ground in front of them. He barked again. A second dog came and stood beside him. The shepherds and the dogs looked at each other for a while then Nahor said: "They have instructions, I think. So do we. Let's go."
The other two nodded in agreement and all three ran off. The brightly lit cloud of angels above them moved towards the valley.
