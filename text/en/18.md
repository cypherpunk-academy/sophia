# Chapter 18

Elisabeth's midwife was the neighbour. She palpated Elisabeth's belly and then said calmly and with an unassuming smile, "He's coming!"
Elizabeth was not in pain, she smiled back and said softly, "I'm ready." Candles burned in the room, standing under clay bowls with various oils burning in them. A bright cloth hung outside the window, dimming the sun but still letting enough in. It was the beginning of summer, the weather had been warm and bright for the last few weeks. And now, at noon, the sun was shining without a cloud in the sky.
The midwife took the little one, lifted him up and then put him on her arm. She looked at him and he made a short cry. Then he opened his eyes a little. "A boy," she said to Elisabeth and placed him on her chest. Elisabath touched him with her hands and he seemed to look at her for a moment, then he fell asleep. Elisabeth breathed calmly and with that raised and lowered him on her chest. She laughed at the midwife and said, "He should be called John!"
Midwife: "We'll ask the father what he means in a moment. There is no one in your whole family called that. How did you come up with that name?" She called her nephew, who had spent the last few days downstairs for necessary errands. The midwife wanted to be with Elisabeth all the time. He came up the stairs and she said to him, "Run to Zacharias, tell him his son is born, and ask him what to name him." The nephew nodded and went off.
He found Zechariah in the temple praying before the altar. He waited until he had finished. As he stood up and turned around the nephew looked at him, "Your son has just been born and my aunt asks what you want to name him."
Zechariah didn't have to think. he took a piece of papyrus and wrote on it, "John." He handed the paper to his nephew. At that moment he grabbed his neck, looked at the nephew in surprise and cleared his throat. He looked around and as everyone in the room looked at him, he said, "From now on, I can answer you again." Everyone was taken aback and came up to him, spoke a few words to him.
The nephew ran back with the papyrus and when the midwife read it, she was very astonished: "He also means John." She showed the paper to Elisabeth. Elisabeth smiled, John had told her himself a few days ago what his name would be. She looked at him and said, "You know exactly how these things go, don't you?" At that moment he opened his eyes a little and made a sucking motion with his mouth. She took him to her breast and he drank.

In the days that followed, many guests came to the house to see John and the Mother and to bring a gift. Sometimes a small queue formed in front of the room upstairs. The priest from Nazareth was also among the guests. He brought a duck as a gift, which Elizabeth gratefully accepted. Then he turned to John, who opened his eyes wider than at others and looked at him long and hard. The priest turned to Elisabeth: "Elisabeth, could I perhaps also speak briefly with Sophia. She is, after all, a child of Nazareth and I have heard that she is here to help you with the birth."
Elizabeth calmly replied, "No, Sophia is no longer here."
Priest: "But I have heard that she has been here for the last three months."
Elisabeth: "That's true, but she's not here now."
Priest: "And where is she?"
Elisabeth: "I don't know. But ask Zacharias."
The priest said goodbye and went down the stairs. In the garden he met Zacharias.
Priest: "Dear priest superior."
Zacharias smiled warmly at him, "What do you desire?"
Priest: "Your advice. I seek a child of Nazareth, Sophia, your niece."
Zacharias: "The daughter of Anna. I have not heard that she is missing. Have you spoken to her mother?"
Priest: "Yes, she said she had been with you for three months."
Zacharias: "Yes, she was. But she left the day before yesterday. She is very independent, you know her."
Priest: "She is not with her mother."
Zacharias: "Then I don't know." He put his hand on his chest and closed his eyes. After a while he said, "She's fine, you don't have to worry."
The priest was about to say something, but Zacharias put a hand on his shoulder, "My son, sometimes you have to trust life." He looked at him urgently.
The priest nodded, thanked him and left the garden. In front of the house he stopped. He was puzzled. A dark cloud clouded his consciousness. He thought hard. After a while he said aloud to himself, "Klophas ... Joseph!"

On his way back, he made a detour into the valley outside Nazareth where Joseph had his farm. He did not take the main road, however, but a path through the olive grove that he remembered from his childhood. When he could see the farm, he stood behind a tree and observed the space in front of the workshop and the house. He could make out Klophas working at his workbench. He watched him for a while, Suddenly he saw Klophas running excitedly towards the house. He couldn't really see what was happening there, so he ran a few trees to the left and there he could briefly see a girl, probably the age of Sophia. Or was that Sophia? He wasn't sure, but he saw Klophas pushing her into the temple cottage and she disappeared inside. It had to be her. There was no other way. He sat down on the floor and clutched his heart, which began to thump loudly. "It's her! She is with Joseph. She's being hidden," he thought.
"Oh, my God," he thought, "two cases in his town in such a short time. This couldn't be true." He had hoped that it would clear up after all and he wouldn't have to go through it all again. In a city where Klophas the Elder had, for so many years, cultivated this forgiving, all-allowing, trusting way of thinking, people were so hard to get to show the consistency and toughness necessary to not allow the bad to spread. And that was so dangerous. He had believed that even while Clophas was alive, it would become apparent that the bad would take over this space that was left for it when people were too lax and tempted people to break the holy commandments. The Jews are the chosen people. Every Jew must understand this. The Messiah will be born into this people, of a Jewish woman. He will save humanity and place Israel at the head of all nations, as His chosen people. And all we have to do for that is to make sure that the woman who will give birth to him would be pure. That's why there were these laws that sometimes had such cruel consequences, cruel but just. That was why he had had to push for Lidia to be stoned to death. He hadn't been able to sleep for weeks afterwards, because of the images that wouldn't leave his mind, and the drama, because of Sophia's scream ... Sophia ... and now Sophia. He closed his eyes. "I can't take this. I don't want to go through this again," he said to himself. But then he clenched a fist. "I have to. It's my job as a priest. God chose me to do it. I can't decide for myself when to obey the laws and when not to." He remained seated until it started to get dark and then made his way back to Nazareth.
