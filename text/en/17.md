# Chapter 17

Three months later.
The priest who had interrogated and sentenced Lidia knocked loudly on Anna's door. He waited and looked at the three priest's assistants standing next to him. He knocked again. Then he called out in a full voice: "Anna, come out! I have been ordered by the elders to find out what is wrong with Sophia. She is a child of our town and no one has seen her in the last three months. Where is she? Show her to us!"
Inside the house, Sophia sat on her bed, leaning against the wall and holding her stomach, which had already thickened a little. She glanced around the room. The curtains of the windows were drawn. Maria was sitting on a chair next to the bed and Anna was standing in front of her with her arms folded. She looked at Sophia, then closed her eyes and shook her head slightly.
The priest grew louder, "I'll come with the blacksmith and then we'll break down your door. We need to see Sophia. Now!"
Anna whispered, "Your life is in danger Sophia, don't you understand? The priest is serious. And you sit here quietly."
Sophia: "I am not in danger. I just have to do what's next. And you're scared. That doesn't help."
Anna: "I'm scared for you!"
Sophia: "The priest can't hurt me."
Anna closed her eyes, "Sophia! You can't say that. Lidia, don't you remember? --- Why did you go away from Elisabeth now?"
Sophia: "Because it was her turn." She looked Anna in the eye. "And probably also because I can't stay in the house when Elisabeth's child is born, all the people come over and I have a belly too. But I don't know that for sure. But I don't have to."
Anna felt a rage rising inside her. She breathed and tried to understand. It was her daughter who was talking like that. She had only been with her aunt. What had happened? She did not realise the gravity of the situation. The anger inside her subsided a little. She thought back to the time when she herself was pregnant with Sophia, how her life suddenly became different, she had much more courage. But she had never remained so calm, in such immediate danger.
The priest shouted from outside, "I'm going to get the blacksmith now, and the goatherd." He banged on the door again with all his might, turned and then went down the street. His assistants followed him. Anna watched them go through a gap in the curtain.
Sophia got up, went into the kitchen, poured some hot water into a bowl and added some herbs. She came back to her room with it. She looked at Anna, "When I've finished the water, we'll continue, I think."
Maria turned her head to her, "I know exactly what you're doing! But isn't it super dangerous? One little doubt and you don't know what's coming next. And you also don't know that you don't know any more."
Anna looked at her confused, "What do you mean, Maria?"
Maria: "It's something between us. You can't understand it so easily. Sophia already understands me."
Anna looked at Sophia, "Sophia, do you understand?"
She nodded.
Anna: "And?"
Sophia: "It's true what Maria says."
Anna sat down on a chair, "Okay, you have your angel with you. He'll tell you what to do."
Sophia shook her head, "My angel is always with me. But I have to find the way on my own, that's important."
Anna got up and went to the window. She saw a shadow approaching the front door. She froze.
Maria: "You are afraid, mother! Don't say anything now. Just be quiet."
Sophia drank a big gulp from the bowl.
There was a loud knock. Four times.
Anna held her breath.
Sophia got up, walked out of the room and opened the front door. She looked in surprise at Klophas, who smiled at her.
Sophia laughed, "Klophas! Come in!"
He walked past her into the house. At that moment, out of the corner of her eye, Sophia saw the priest coming up the road with the blacksmith and the goatherd.
Sophia: "Klophas! You have to help us. The priest must not come in here right away. We need a little time. Quick! Think of something, you must distract him."
Klophas looked at her urgently, then after a few moments said, "Okay, I've got something." He ran outside, towards the men.
Sophia ran into her room, "Mother, you said you were going to slaughter the goat this guard. You would have to do that now. Right now!"
Anna looked at her in amazement and nodded. Sophia ran into the yard to the animals, crawled into the hare cage and lay down under the hay in the far corner. Maria came up behind her and covered the spot with more hay. The rabbits approached and started chewing on it.
Anna tied an apron around herself, got the goat out of its pen and hugged it, then gave it to Maria, who stroked it all over her body and then hugged it too. Anna pulled a cloth wrap from a compartment next to the door to the house. Wrapped in it was a sharp butcher's knife. She took it, checked that it was sharp enough and knelt down with it next to the goat. Then she grabbed the goat by the horns and gently pushed it down so that it had to lie down. As it lay down, she slit the goat's throat and gently pressed its head to the ground with her hand. She whispered a few words in the goat's ear. Mary lay half on top of her and embraced her. So they paused for a while while the blood flowed over the floor into the gutter. The goat remained calm the whole time.

Clophas ran towards the priest and called to him from some distance: "Priest! Priest! I am Klophas. I have a question."
The priest came up to him, "I know who you are. I also know where you got your name. But we don't have time now."
Klophas: "Please! I won't be long."
The priest stopped and looked at him impatiently, "Yes?"
Klophas took posture, put a hand on his chest and said firmly, "I want to get married!" He looked the priest in the eye.
Priest: "You are 15 years old. There's still time."
Clophas: "No, it hasn't! I have to do this now."
Priest: "And who do you want to marry?"
Clophas: "Mary of Nazareth, daughter of Anna of Nazareth."
The priest smiled, "Have you already told Anna?"
Clophas: "How could I, without your consent? I must first ask a priest if it is possible."
Priest: "Not now!" He tried to push Klophas aside, but he resisted.
Klophas: "It's urgent!"
Priest: "Urgent?"
Klophas looked down at the ground, "Urgent!"
The priest looked at him with interest, "What happened?"
Clophas: "I walked up a meadow with Mary."
The priest: "And then?"
Clophas: "I took her by the hand and we went into an olive grove."
Priest: "Okay, and then?"
Clophas: "Then we rested from the climb and sat side by side."
The priest looked at him grimly.
Klophas looked up in the air.
Priest: "What happened then?"
Klophas: "I fell in love --- we then walked back."
The priest exhaled loudly and closed his eyes.
He opened them again, "WHY IS THAT URGENT?"
Klophas: "Because I love her. I cannot wait. I don't want to keep her in the dark. Not a minute."
Priest: "You were with her, weren't you? You were lying together, weren't you?"
Klophas looked at him in amazement and said nothing.
Priest: "This is very serious! Then you must marry her."
Klophas looked at him questioningly.
Priest: "So it is true? You have lain with her?"
Klophas remained silent.
Priest: "This is very serious! You remember Lidia? You don't want Maria to meet the same fate."
Klophas took half a step back and said indignantly, "I have not lain with her. Not at all. Why do you mean that? I would never do that before the wedding."
The priest looked at him in confusion.
Clophas: "That is an unfair and dangerous accusation, and a false one at that! If my parents were still alive ..." He sat down on the ground and clasped his knees.
The blacksmith reared up beside the priest, "Are we going or not? I still have work to do."
The priest pointed his finger at Klophas, "I am sorry if I have accused you falsely. I will make a sacrifice for it on the Alter. If Anna agrees, you can marry Maria, for all I care, but she won't. Now let's go to Anna's, you can come with me and ask her then and there."
The priest walked off towards the front door. Blacksmith, goatherd and Klophas behind. He was about to knock when Klophas called from behind, "It's open. Go in!"
The priest opened the door in amazement and entered the house. Inside, all the doors were open; there was no one there. He opened the door to the backyard and there he saw Anna kneeling on the ground with a blood-covered apron and bloody hands. She was in the process of cutting off the goat's head. Maria was kneeling beside it, still holding the goat's back. He looked at the two of them in confusion.
Anna laid the goat's head on its side and then, facing the goat, said a prayer.
The blacksmith nudged the priest, "What now?"
The priest loudly to Anna: "Anna! Where is Sophia?"
Anna looked at him urgently, "Can't you see we're in the middle of a slaughter? You are welcome to come here and say a blessing. Or don't you want to do that as our priest here in Nazareth?"
The priest came and reluctantly knelt before the goat, raised one hand and spoke a few words. Then everyone remained silent for a few minutes until the blood was completely drained.
The priest and Anna stood up. Anna said to Maria, "Go ahead and start sharing." She took the knife from the floor and handed it to her. Then she turned to the priest and said, "Sophia has spent the last three months at her great aunt's to help her with the child that is being born now. That's why no one has seen her."
The priest looked at her in amazement, "I know your aunt is pregnant. We thought she was there and checked. She wasn't there."
Anna said with anger, "Of course she was there! She was there for the last three months. Have you been there yourself and seen this with your own eyes?"
Priest: "We have received news that Sophia is not in Cana."
Anna: "Then it was false news!"
The priest looked at her sceptically.
Anna looked back angrily, "How many times did my first husband, Clophas, lie in his whole life?"
Priest: "Not once."
Anna: "And I don't lie either. NOW GET OUT OF MY SIGHT!" She wiped the blood on her hands on her apron.
The priest looked at her. His head ran dark red. After a few moments he turned and walked away. The blacksmith and the goatherd followed him.
Klophas looked at Anna and waited until he heard the front door fly shut with a loud crash. Then he laughed.
Anna started too and infected Maria with it. The hay in the hare cage rustled and Sophia came out grinning.
Sophia: "Thank you, Klophas! You live up to your name."
Klophas bowed elegantly and Maria giggled.
Sophia: "Why are you here, anyway?"
Klophas: "Joseph sent me to bring you to us. He thinks everything is ready now, including him."
Sophia laughed: "Yes, that sounds good. I'll be happy to come along. When?"
Klophas: "Tonight, when the priest is asleep."
Sophia nodded, Anna shook her head and Maria smiled admiringly at Klophas. He turned to Anna.
He went down on his knees in front of her. She looked at him in amazement.
Klophas: "Anna! Widow of the great Clophas, mother of Sophia and Maria, I ask you, with the permission of the priest of this city, for permission to marry your daughter Maria." Maria's eyes grew wide, her mouth opened and then she smiled again. Anna took a step back, "Klophas! Are you serious?
Klophas: "Yes!"
Anna: "You are 15 years old!"
Klophas: "15 years 7 months and 9 days. That's almost ..."
Anna: "You are 15 years old. Marriage is no fun."
Klophas' eyes darkened, he looked at her urgently, "I'm Klophas, I don't joke with that sort of thing. How many jokes did your first husband make with something like this in his life?"
Anna: "None."
Klophas: "I bear his name with dignity!"
Anna: "You are 15 years old. Maria is 12! You have to wait until she is 16. Then you can come back and ask again."
Klophas: "Good! I will do that." He gave Maria a radiant look. She smiled warmly back.
