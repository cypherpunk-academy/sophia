# Chapter 23

Elisabeth took John out of bed and showed him the rising sun at the window. He wanted to grab it, tried a few times and then stretched his legs.
Elisabeth: "Do you want to stand yourself? That won't work yet. You are only half a year old." Johannes stretched his legs even more and Elisabeth carefully placed him on the floor. Then she let go of him and he stood, himself, laughing at her. Then he reached out a hand to her and she clasped it. At that moment, Johannes took a step forward, still a little shaky, but the next few went better. He pulled Elisabeth towards the stairs.
Elisabeth: "No, you can't climb stairs yet. Johannes pulled her harder. She went with him and sure enough, he took steps and they became more confident and sure. They went down the stairs together and then into the garden. Johannes pointed towards the sun and made some unintelligible sounds, as if he wanted to say something.

Klophas came panting back to the exercise yard with two large empty buckets attached to either end of a stick. He had previously carried them full of water up all the steps of a four-storey house and poured them down from the roof at the top. Now he stood slightly bent forward in front of a broad-shouldered Roman instructor. "STOP! Freshman," he roared at him. Klophas straightened up in a flash and looked him powerfully in the eye.
Instructor: "Well, tired?"
Klophas replied in a joyful tone, "No. Not a bit."
Instructor: "No is enough information. THEN TWO MORE BUCKETS!"
Klophas ran to the well, filled the buckets, and shouldered them both. Then he ran back towards the house.
The instructor took a few deep breaths.
Joachim slapped him hard on the shoulder from behind: "Well? Can you get him small? How much has he got?"
The instructor didn't move, "That will come. He has twenty. How come he doesn't even get a whiff of a bad mood?"
Joachim: "Because you're too weak for him, maybe?"
The instructor looked at Joachim out of the corner of his eye.
Joachim: "No. You understand that when you look at what he experienced as a child. Besides, he's in love ..."
The instructor pointed his finger at Joachim and nodded, "That's it!"

Mary, who was now officially called Sophia, looked at the sign above the front door, it said in Latin; "Joachim Lazarus Family, Roman Citizen" and bore a seal of Herod. She smiled. Word had spread quickly in the village about the name change, there were also many different stories as to why it had happened. The one that found the most followers was that the names had been entered incorrectly by the Romans in Jerusalem and that they had been adapted to that. So far, no one had asked about a pregnant Sophia. But the priest, who now also had to call her Sophia, seemed a little more afraid around him when he passed her house. He was probably still impressed by what had happened. Anna came up behind him and put her arms around Maria. So they looked at the sign together for a while.
Anna: "What do you think Maria is doing right now?"
Maria closed her eyes and put both hands on her chest, "She's in good spirits. But exhausted, I think."

Sophia, who was now called Maria, walked behind Joseph between the ox and the donkey. She could no longer feel her feet. She tapped him on the shoulder, he turned and she smiled at him. She pointed to a resting bench on the side of the path and Josef led the animals there. They sat down. It had become even colder that day, Sophia's lips were slightly blue. Josef saw this and offered her his coat. But she refused, saying it was too heavy for her. Josef took her in his arms.
Sophia: "Ohh! That's nice!" She snuggled up to him. "You have blue lips too." She pulled him closer to her.
Joseph: "It must be the coldest day."
Sophia: "Everything is as it should be." A shiver ran through her.
Joseph: "Is that what your angel says?"
Sophia: "No, he's not talking to me much right now. But I think that means everything is all right."
Joseph nodded, "It's not that far now. It's lunchtime now. I think we will be in Bethlehem before dark."
Sophia: "That's nice! And there?"
Joseph: "I know an innkeeper. We used to play together as children. He will take us in."
Sophia: "I can stand it for a few more hours. I'm already imagining a nice, warm fire and soup and fresh bread."
Josef pressed her closer to him.
Josef: "Soon we will have made it."
