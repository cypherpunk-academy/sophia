# Chapter 12

Sophia sat on the edge of her bed. She was alone in the room. She leaned forward slightly and looked sideways at the stool by the window.
Sophia: "You mean like this? --- A little more hollow back? --- Until I feel the energy flowing forward out to my chest?" --- Ohhh, yes, now I feel something! That's nice!"
Sophia held out in this posture for a few moments and then turned towards the chair. It was empty on the outside, but just as she had seen Lidia on the stool a few days before, she now saw a woman of about 30 in a beautiful, airy dress, a free smile and sparkling eyes full of energy and humour.
Sophia: "Show me something else! --- good --- I know I need to let my hair grow a little more ... --- okay, just for that." Anna propped her hands on the bed a little behind her back and let her head fall back. "Chest up a little more --- yes, I understand, ohh, that feels nice too."
The door opened and Anna came in. Sophia turned abruptly to look at her. She looked at the stool for a moment, then back at her mother.
Anna: "How are you doing now? Have you been outside today?"
Sophia: "Yes, I went to feed the animals and fetch new water. And yes, it's going quite well."
Anna sat down on the bed with her and stroked her hair, "Sophia, we have a visitor. He's a friend of Zacharias. I'd like you to talk to him about the stoning. He might be able to find out if you're missing something because of it."
Sophia: "I'm not missing anything that I could just get. Or can you make people stop forgetting themselves so easily, and killing other people? I don't want to go out there because there are people everywhere who took part in that."
Anna: "There were few from our village."
Sophia: "The ones who did it are still there. No one sends them away."
Anna: "It's not that easy either. You can't just do away with the rules and laws like that, you'd have to be king yourself and spend a lifetime preparing yourself so you know what you're doing and don't make rules and laws that are harmful."
Sophia: "I don't want to go out there!"
Anna: "Maybe Zachariah's friend knows something about how to make people not kill each other."
Sophia looked at her questioningly out of the corner of her eye.
"Come on, he's waiting outside with the animals."
Sophia: "Is that really what you want, Mother, for me to talk to him?"
Anna nodded.
Sophia rose from the bed a little reluctantly and walked past her mother out the door and into the yard.
At the bunny cage, Josef stood with his back to her and stroked a bunny he had placed on his hand. Sophia looked at him. She was amazed.
After a while, Josef turned and looked at her with a warm gaze: "Oh, hello!"
Sophia looked back sceptically, "You're not scared at all around you. I can't see anything at all. Even my mother always has a little."
Joseph nodded.
Sophia: "That doesn't mean you're not afraid, it's just hidden right now. But how do you do it? You're in a strange place, you don't know me ..."
Joseph smiled and held the bunny out to her: "This one isn't afraid either. He seems to be doing well here with you."
Sophia: "That one was born dead and then came back. Then they become like this."
Joseph nodded.
Sophia: "Anna thinks you might know what to do to stop people losing themselves and killing each other."
Josef laughed, "Did she say that? --- No, I don't know that."
Sophia: "You killed my friend. But it says in the law, Thou shalt not kill."
Joseph: "Yes, as you say, they lost each other and then this can happen."
Sophia looked at him.
Joseph: "That's what fear does. They get lost in their fear."
Sophia: "I've already figured all that out myself. Do you also have something new? Something I don't know yet?"
Joseph: "Yes."
Sophia: "Yes? --- And?"
Joseph: "You haven't asked yet."
Sophia: "I asked: do you also have something I don't know yet?"
Joseph: "And I answered."
An anger rose in Sophia. Joseph looked at her. Then he sat down on the floor with the bunny in his hand. Sophia was confused. After a while she came closer and stood directly in front of him.
Sophia: "How do you do that? You are still not afraid. Not even when I talk to you like this. Adults usually get a little nervous, or they stand up, or they turn away. You just stay calm with me."
Joseph closed his eyes and breathed quietly, "I ... maybe I'm content with myself, with the world and everything around me, and stay that way, even if you say what you say. After all, there is no danger. You can see everything of me if you want to. I don't have to hide anything. And I don't have to achieve anything in particular or be a certain way either." He opened his eyes and looked at her, "But you're right: when I meet people, there's a pretty big risk that I'll end up in some kind of fear at some point. Fear is contagious, in a way. But here with you in your house, the danger is not great. You have almost no fear."
Sophia: "Almost none? What am I afraid of?"
Joseph: "Of you going out of the house and realising that people are the way they are and that that's okay, because it's okay to be imperfect, because everyone is imperfect. And that doesn't fit with you wanting to stop them from doing that again. And that's where you think wrong somewhere and then you get scared and you don't want to leave the house."
Sophia looked at him in amazement. After a while she sat down in front of him. She put her hands in front of her face, "You'll do it again. I know it. --- BUT HOW CAN YOU SAY THAT IS OK?" she said with anger in her voice.
Joseph replied quietly, "It's not okay. But they are okay. It's just what they do that's not okay. They don't realise that."
Sophia: "But that's no solution, they're getting more and more entangled in their lies."
Joseph: "Yes."
Sophia: "Just like that, yes, and done, that's it? You're so old and you understand a lot and you're not afraid and you see what's happening and you just let it be?"
Joseph: "Yes."
Sophia: "That's cowardly. You give up because you think there's nothing you can do. --- But you sit here and you're not desperate, you have a warmth that makes me want to hug you..." She closed her eyes for a few moments and then shook her head. "So you believe that people are not lost, you believe that there is salvation?"
Joseph: "Yes."
Sophia held her hands over her eyes, "But how?"
Joseph smiled at her, "I don't know." He waited a few moments and then looked Sophia straight in the eye. "That wouldn't be good if I knew, because then I'd be telling you and people what they can do. --- I would do that, I know that. And that's why it's good that I don't know."
Sophia took her hands away from her eyes, "So everyone can figure it out for themselves."
Joseph nodded, "It only has power when people come to it themselves. Otherwise it's just wisdom. --- Only most people can't come to it themselves.... actually no one can. --- You and Maria have understood something through the stoning. Also because you were able to talk to Lidia. Will you ever forget that again?"
Sophia: "No, never."
Joseph: "And so everyone who has understood something can learn how to do it differently. But the vast majority of people can't. They may be able to understand, but then they can't learn from it, because then they would have to make up their own minds. And they can't do that. They can only do that with practical, external things. They cannot think something that is only internal and that others around them are not also thinking. They can't make up their own new thoughts, they get what they think from others, and them too. And that's where we don't get anywhere, that's where we get stuck ... That's how we humans are. We would first have to understand that real thinking is not abstract and only produces shadow thoughts, but living, powerful thoughts that can really unhinge the world, and how these thoughts can heal everything, every illness. If I make the exact thoughts that I need for myself, that fit into my life, into my past, then they can heal. But nobody can do that for me. And that's what we humans can't do, I don't think we can yet."
Sophia: "The Messiah! He comes to show us humans how we can think anew, how we can think for ourselves, how we can recreate living thoughts."
Joseph looked at Sophia in amazement and laughed, "Where from? ... Where did you get that now?"
Sophia: "It just came. It's like that with me sometimes. But do you think so?"
Joseph nodded, "Yes, I think so too --- we can't do it alone. We need someone to show us, to model for us how to think beyond our limitations, how to think beyond family, to think and live beyond our people, beyond death. The Messiah comes for all people in all nations. And he shows us how to think new thoughts that fit our own lives exactly, that heal our lives and the lives of those around us."
Sophia: "And when is he coming?"
Joseph laughed, then both said at the same time, "At the right time!" They both laughed and looked at each other.
Sophia: "Now I understand that you say you can't do anything."
Joseph nodded.
Sophia: "And when the Messiah comes, he will drive out all the demons too, won't he?"
Joseph smiled, "When they are no longer needed, then they will disappear, but it will take a long time. But in the end they will go. Lilith, for example ..."
Sophia looked at him out of the corner of her eye, "You saw me talking to Lilith earlier, didn't you?"
Joseph nodded, "Yes, when I came in. I know her well. She's often with me."
Sophia looked at him in amazement, "Why? I thought she was there for women."
Josef: "You feel good inside when you move the way she pretends and think the way she tells you to, don't you?"
Sophia nodded.
Josef: "The special thing about what Lilith does is that it doesn't only work in you, but also in others. Especially in men, they can experience an even stronger energy in them through the movements than you can. It pulls them there with great force, and that opens the imagination organ in the middle of the head, and so new thoughts can arise. I love to look at them. Many men then prefer to possess the woman and use their imagination for what they could increase with her, I prefer to have new thoughts."
Sophia marvelled at him, "I'll have to ask her about that sometime." She propped her hands behind her back and tilted her head back carefully, her hair falling over her shoulder. All the while she looked closely at Joseph.
He looked at her quietly for a while and then laughed: "You've learned something already."
Sophia: "And you're right. I saw that you suddenly had a lot more energy in the middle of your head. It works. And did you get new thoughts?"
Josef: "Yes, I did. Next week I'm getting a new ox on my farm. Would you like to welcome him to my farm together with your sister? Klophas will be there too."
Sophia: "Klophas? My mother's former husband?"
Joseph laughed: "No, a Klophas of flesh and blood and about your age. This is my apprentice."
Sophia: "Yes, I'd love to come. And Maria, too, I'm sure."
