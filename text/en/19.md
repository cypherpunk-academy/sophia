# Chapter 19

Klophas went to the temple cottage, in front of which there was now a bench to the right of the door, protected from outside view by some large plants placed in front of it. Sitting on the bench, Sophia caught one of the rays of sunlight that shone on the spot behind the house at this time of day. She stood up. Klophas made a hand signal that she should not join him.
Klophas: "Sophia, you have to be careful! If you come out too far, you can be seen from the olive grove across the road."
Sophia: "You watch me!" She smiled at Klophas. "But it's not dangerous. Who's in this olive grove anyway." She stepped out a little. Klophas put his hands on her shoulders, "Only so far! Joseph told me to be strict with you."
Sophia: "And I tell you, you don't have to be strict with me. I have my way. I can't get lost." She faltered and looked to her left in surprise, then to her right.
Klophas: "What is it?"
Sophia: "Something is happening. I'd better go to the temple cottage."
Just then Joseph came up the path: "Quick! Sophia, go in there. People are coming and I don't think with good intentions."
Klophas looked at him with wide eyes. Sophia disappeared through the door of the temple cottage. Klophas walked across the courtyard until he saw a group of about ten men coming out the way down the valley. He could not yet make out who it was.
Joseph said to Klophas, "Just work normally. That's probably the best thing to do now."
Klophas: "How many are there?"
Josef: "Twelve people."
Klophas: "Okay." He stood at the workbench, took a half-finished window frame and put it on the table. He took a file and started working on one side of it. Joseph stood in the middle of the courtyard square, arms folded, facing the group that would soon be coming up the path to the courtyard. He recognised the priest from Nazareth, walking away in front with another man. Joseph breathed in and out deeply. When the group was still a stone's throw away from Joseph, the priest and the other man, left and right stepped aside and behind them two men in Roman uniforms appeared. They had not been seen before. Joseph closed his eyes for a moment. Klophas looked at the soldiers from his workbench, fascinated. It was the first time he had seen Roman soldiers. Mary's father was one, but he had only heard that before.
The priest approached Joseph and stopped in front of him. The Roman soldiers stood beside him. Joseph and the priest looked at each other for a long time.
Joseph: "Greetings, priest! What do you want this time? Am I still not living far enough away from the city for you?"
Priest: "It is about more serious things, this time."
Joseph; "So serious that you have to bring two Roman soldiers?"
Priest; "So serious."
Joseph looked at him in surprise.
Priest; "It's about adultery."
Joseph did not take his eyes off the priest; "I have no wife, I never had one. Why do you come to me for adultery?"
Priest: "There is a suspicion that a child is growing here that was conceived outside of marriage."
Joseph: "With me?"
Priest: "It's about Sophia. She is with you!" He looked at Joseph urgently.
Joseph looked back, "Why should she be with me?"
Priest: "It is the only place she can be, except with her mother or Elizabeth."
Joseph turned and called to Caiaphas, "Caiaphas, is Sophia, the daughter of Anna here."
Caiaphas called back, "Yes, we are keeping her hidden in the temple cottage. You know that."
The priest looked at Joseph in surprise, "Yes, really? A clophas never lies, you know that."
Joseph waited a short while and then laughed out loud, "Yes, that's right! We keep them hidden there. You're welcome to look."
Priest: "The situation is serious! I don't like your jokes. It's a matter of life and death." He turned and indicated two men to search in the barn, two in the carpenter's workshop, two in the house. The rest, except the soldiers, he sent to the temple cottage.
Joseph asked him: "And why soldiers? Why are they interfering here."
One of the soldiers looked at him and said, "You are from the house of David, right? Herod has asked that all male descendants of the previous royal lines be supervised."
Joseph looked at him thoughtfully.
Soldier: "If Sophia has a child by you, it can be an heir to the throne. Then ..."
Joseph: "Are you going to kill it? I thought you weren't doing that any more? I thought that was over?"
Soldier: "Then it will be registered. Nothing more."
Joseph: "But this one," he pointed at the priest, "would have his mother stoned to death before the child is even born."
Soldier: "We do not interfere with your customs."
Joseph: "... as long as she is not a Roman citizen."
Soldier: "As long as she is not a Roman citizen. Otherwise she would be protected by Roman law. In occupied territories we only exchange rulers, demand taxes and recruit soldiers. Otherwise, we do not interfere with the local culture. We even help sometimes ... like now."
The men returned from each building and all reported finding nothing.
The priest's face turned red: "THAT CAN'T BE! I saw them a few days ago, here in the courtyard square." He pointed in the direction of the temple house. "Did you look carefully at everything in there?"
The men who had been looking in the temple cottage nodded, "She's not in there. It's not that big. You must have made a mistake."
The soldier looked at him grimly, "You said she must be here! You said you saw her?"
Priest: "I have seen her."
Soldier: "Then show her to us! We came all the way up here with her."
The priest looked at Klophas and called out, "Klophas, come here!"
Klophas put the file on the table and came to the priest. He greeted everyone and marvelled at the Roman uniforms. He wanted to touch one with his hand and asked the soldier, "May I?"
The soldier shook his head.
Priest: "Your name is Klophas. You are not lying, are you? Like the great Klophas. You said earlier that you were hiding Sophia. Where is she?"
Clophas: "In the temple cottage."
Priest: "Show her to me!"
Clophas: "No."
The soldiers looked at the priest in surprise. He became uncertain.
Priest: "I demand that you fetch them! As your priest of your city."
Clophas: "By what law do you command me to fetch her?"
The priest looked to the ground, and was silent.
Soldier to priest: "Why don't you go and see for yourself? Your people don't seem to obey you."
The priest looked at the soldier and then at Klophas, pushed him aside and walked towards the temple cottage.
In the cottage he stood in the middle of the large room and listened. He looked along the walls, the altar. He saw nothing conspicuous. He looked into the small room next to the big one, also along the walls and on the floor. Then he went back to the big room, examined the floor. Stamped in various places. He looked out through the small window opposite the door. There lay the great pile of wood for the carpentry. An angel stood, invisible to the priest, on the left side of the altar wall, holding his hands on a crack in the wall. There was a flap there, covering a passage that led to the woodpile outside. But the priest could not look at it because the angel was standing there. He thought. With a wave of his hand, the angel blew away any thought the priest had of going towards the woodpile. The priest became nervous. He did not understand. She had to be here. He could find no clue. He looked again at everything he had looked at before.
A soldier came into the room and also examined the walls, the floor in both rooms. He checked the altar wall. After he had looked at everything, he said, "She is not here!"
Priest: "She must be here. Klophas does not lie. Never."
Soldier: "All boys that age lie sometimes. And priests lie sometimes too." He looked at him threateningly.
Priest: "I have seen them! Believe me!"
Soldier: "And I don't know if what you say is true, even if you repeat it. I will make a note in the register."
Priest: "NO, please don't! Please ..."
Soldier: "Then show it to us. We came out here because you said she was here expecting a child from Joseph. And I don't see that. Roman soldiers cannot be used with impunity by Jewish priests for their own purposes."
The priest went down on his knees.
The soldier gave him a contemptuous look and left the room.
The priest sank to the floor and held his hands over his head.
