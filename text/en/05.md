# Chapter 5

Zechariah stood in his festive priestly robe behind the heavy curtain at the entrance to the Great Temple in Jerusalem. He heard the crowd outside talking loudly together, praying, greeting each other, shouting in confusion.... He closed his eyes and breathed in and out a few times. Then he looked ahead, nodded to the left and a temple servant pushed aside the curtain. Zechariah strode out. The crowd quietened. A few moments later, everything was dead silent. Zechariah took a few more steps up to the grand staircase and looked over the crowd. Then he spread his arms and closed his eyes. After five long minutes, he raised both arms to the sky, made a sign of blessing over the crowd and went back into the temple. Inside, he heard that the crowd was still standing there in silence.
He went to the room at the end of the temple, the Holy of Holies, where he would prepare the incense offering. The temple servants had arranged a selection of the things the people had brought on an offering table. The large fire bowl was burning with hot embers. He turned to the table and was about to take the first offering, a lamb's head, when he felt a bright energy in his back that took over more and more of the room. He thought, "Oh! An angel!" and slowly turned around. He was startled and went down on his knees. In front of him stood a three-metre-tall angel with a luminous robe of gold and red sable. He wore emeralds around his collar and at the ends of his sleeves. His breastplate shone far brighter than the fire burning immediately behind him. Zechariah crouched on the ground and trembled. The angel said in a dark, loud but warm voice: "Don't be afraid, Zacharias! --- Jehovah sends me --- to you --- to tell you that Elizabeth will soon conceive a child --- and you shall name him John." Zacharias, still huddled, said in a brittle voice, "But my wife is too old to have a child." "Nothing is impossible for Jehovah. For your untrust, I will take your voice until the child is born." Zechariah looked up cautiously. The angel had disappeared. He grabbed his neck. He tried to bring out a word, but he couldn't, his larynx wasn't responding. He wasn't in pain, he didn't feel sick or weak, nothing was pressing on him, his larynx just wasn't responding.
He straightened up. Went back to the offering table, took the lamb's head, placed it on a wire rack and brought both to the fire. When all the offerings were in the fire and he had watched the smoke for a while, he walked back towards the curtain. He nodded to the temple servant and he drew the curtain. Zechariah walked through, towards the crowd. Now he should have made his speech, about what he had seen in the smoke, what thoughts came to him, how the coming year would be, so it had been for hundreds of years. He knew of no instance where a priest had stood up here after the incense offering and said nothing. He had spent weeks preparing for this, he knew exactly what he had seen and wanted to say, but now he could say nothing. He raised his arms above the crowd and then pointed his hand to his neck and made a gesture that he could not speak. Tears stood in his eyes. After a few moments he turned and slowly walked back into the temple.

One week later.
Elisabeth was waiting for him at the gate of her house. He came, dropped his bundle of luggage and they embraced for a long time. Elisabeth asked him nothing, she had heard what had happened. The news had spread like wildfire. They went into the house together. He sat down at the desk, took one of the scrolls and began to read. She hugged him from behind and rested her head on his shoulder. He gently stroked her head with his hand and began to cry.
