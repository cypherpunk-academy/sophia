# Chapter 22

Simeon was sitting on a resting bench on a path somewhere in the mountains around Bethlehem. The sun was just rising. It was cold, winter was also just around the corner. There had been no frost yet, which was also quite rare in this area, but this year it felt like it might happen. He meditated. Every now and then he looked up and glanced to the left and right, but nothing was happening that morning, just a few birds chirping in the bushes around him. Otherwise it was silent.
"There!" thought Simeon, "was that sheep bleating in the distance?" He listened. "Once more! --- They are coming," he thought, "how beautiful! The soon to be celebrities who knew not yet of their fate."
A little later he heard quick footsteps coming from the direction of the sheep-baiting. Someone was hurrying down the path. He kept a lookout for him. A young man with only one shoe, a laced Roman sandal, ran towards him ... and past him. But then he stopped and turned to face him.
Simeon thought, "Okay, that must be Amon. After all, he would never leave his one sandal for anyone else." He smiled at him.
Amon: "Who are you? I had a feeling I was supposed to stop."
Simeon: "Amon! You don't know me anymore?"
Amon went down on his knees, "Forgive me, Simeon, I was in such a hurry. --- Simeon. You are here. Thank you for all you have taught me. I have forgotten nothing and taken everything to heart. You are the greatest master I know." He bowed. Simeon also bowed.
Amon: "No, you cannot bow to me. That would only be mockery. I am a shepherd's assistant ... Shepherd now. And you are a great master."
Simeon bowed once more.
Amon: "You are making fun of me, why?"
Simeon: "No, I'm not after that. I wouldn't bow down to other shepherds, but I would bow down to you and your two brothers."
Amon: "That makes no sense. We are ordinary shepherds, all three of us, there is no difference at all between us and other ordinary shepherds. But I have to run to the Dort now, a sheep has hurt itself and we need an ointment."
Simeon: "I will wait for you here."
Amon shook his head and ran off.
Two hours later he found Simeon on the same bench, surrounded by the flock of sheep. He had the injured sheep on his lap, Nahor was kneeling in front of him holding the injured leg in his hands. Simeon was sewing the last stitch on the wound and spoke a few words into the sheep's ear.
Amon out of breath, "Here's the ointment. Do you still need it?"
Simeon: "By all means." He smelled it, "With arnica and calendula. It's very good." He carefully rubbed the ointment on the wound. Then they released the sheep.
Nahor, Gallum and Amon sat in a semicircle around Simeon and marvelled at him.
Nahor: "So you are the high priest who approached Amon in Jerusamlem and turned his head? Why did you do that?"
Simeon: "Addressed him or turned his head?"
Nahor: "Approached. He talks about you all the time. Do you do that often, that you approach simple shepherds on the road?"
Simeon: "First of all, have you ever tried to turn Amon's head? Or dissuade him from anything he's convinced of."
Gallum: "You can't."
Nahor: "No, I haven't managed that yet. What's your trick?"
Simeon: "No trick. Every question you have attracts its answer. And Amon has a mighty lot of questions and always pulls on them pretty hard. That's why he gets a lot of answers that fit, and it's hard to get him away from that. But it takes time for him to understand the answers and I, I can just read them. They are there in the space around him, or in his atmosphere, or in the invisible world, the ideas that surround us all. And that's where I unpack my net and catch the appropriate answers to his questions. And he likes that."
Nahor and Gallum marvelled at him with open mouths.
Simeon: "It works so well with Amon because he just is who he is. He doesn't mix anything into his questions, for example, himself. He doesn't want to be successful and shine, or become wise, or better than others, he just wants to know. And then the right answers want to come to him."
Nahor and Gallum nodded, "That's right, he just wants to know."
Nahor: "Then the special thing why you approached him was that he just is who he is? Really? What's special about that?"
Simeon: "To be as you are is to be pure."
Gallum: "Pure, like the mother of the Messiah who is to come?"
Simeon: "Yes, like that."
Nahor: "Ho ho. Our Amon! He is pure. He's really like what he is. Look at him Gallum ..."
Amon spread his arms and said, "I am as I am."
Gallum stood on his head, spread his legs, crossed his arms in front of his chest and balanced. Very carefully he said, "I am also as I am!"
Nahor and Amon laughed, "Yes, you are like you too."
Amon: "Hey, Simeon! Now you already have two pure ones here."
Simeon laughed out loud. Gallum dropped backwards and sat down again as before.
Gallum: "Simeon, why did you come up here?"
Simeon looked at all three and then said, "The Messiah is coming soon. This winter. On the coldest day."
All three looked at him in horror. None of them moved.
Simeon stood up, "That's what I wanted to tell you. That's why I came here. I have to go on now."
He set off on his way. The three looked after him, then looked at each other. For a long time, no one said a word.
Amon: "The Messiah is coming! What shall we do now?"
Gallum: "What is there for us to do? We are shepherds."
Amon: "That the Messiah is coming is understood only by the common people. That's what Simeon told me."
Everyone was silent again for a while.
Nahor: "If they want us to do something, they'll have to be more specific than Simeon was just now. Then they'll have to send an angel or something."
Everyone nodded.
