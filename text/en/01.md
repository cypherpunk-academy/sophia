# Chapter 1

The old priest walked a few last steps and then wearily dropped onto the bench at the side of the road. He had already seen it at the beginning of the climb up to the city and had looked at it again and again with longing. He took a few deep breaths, folded his hands and gave thanks aloud that he had survived this long journey. He closed his eyes ... a bright light flashed through him. The landscape around him had changed: The hill up to the city had disappeared, the city had gained a city wall, with a gate and guards in front of it. A wide river flowed around the city. The priest rose and walked a few steps towards the bank, where on his left some women were washing clothes and on his right, upstream, a boat loaded with wood was coming towards him. Behind him he suddenly heard the clatter of armour. He was startled, turned around and saw a shadow flying towards him - a sword! It struck him on the neck and penetrated deeply. He fell to his knees, grasped the wound and felt the warm blood flowing through his hand. Then he lost consciousness and a moment later found himself floating a few metres above his own body, which lay curled in a pool of blood.

Screams from children snapped him out of it. He sat back on the bench at the entrance to the town, breathing heavily. He looked down the hill and recognised far down the road a woman pulling a handcart and two children jumping around her. Two girls! He laughed out loud and looked with narrowed eyes. He recognised Anna and her two daughters Sophia and Maria - and laughed again, shaking his head and thinking, "Hardly, I'm here ...". He put his hands on his chest and closed his eyes.

Again a light flashed through him. He was in an image again, like in a dream, only much more real, as if he were part of it. Two women were kneeling there side by side, right in front of him, writhing in pain. One had her arm over the other, who was trembling all over her body. She slowly began to lift her head to look up at something that seemed to be behind the man. He turned and saw a huge cross, erect, very close in front of him, the place where the beam of the cross had been driven into the ground. He saw drops of blood. His gaze wandered upwards, to the bleeding feet of a man, which had been hammered to the beam with a wooden nail ...
A close cry of a child startled the old man, the image disappeared, he looked at the girls who had come closer in the meantime. Sophia was just helping Maria out of a ditch at the side of the road, laughing. Her little sister looked at her grimly, swung at her, pushed her away and ran off. She came straight at him, Sophia after her. Maria jumped behind the bench and Sophia came towards him, watching closely what Maria was doing.
Sophia stopped and looked at him in surprise, "Hello Simon!" she called, looking him in the eye for a moment, she laughed and then pushed off with both hands at his knees to catch Maria trying to get away from the bench. The two of them ran off.
"Welcome Simeon!" called Anna, he turned to her and smiled. She sat down next to him. He put his hand on his chest. They looked into each other's eyes for a long time. Then Anna laughed, "It's good to have you back!" He nodded, "And what a nice coincidence to see the three of you first." Anna smiled, moving closer to him. "And it's still the same reason you walked the three days from Jerusalem to here?" "Of course! The two girls are getting strong, aren't they?" "Mh mh."
"Do you have a teacher for them yet?" he asked. "No, I don't think they need one. For what? Life has everything they need to learn. And I'll teach them the rest." Simeon looked at her thoughtfully. She asked him, "Do you see anything about a teacher in your paintings?" He closed his eyes for a few moments. "No, not clearly. An older man is there sometimes. But I haven't seen his face yet. But it comes as it must anyway." "You can't tell me any more, can you?" "Do you have a question?" He closed his eyes. "Yes. Is she going to get married?" "Yes, she will, but first she'll go through the same thing you went through." Anna turned pale in the face and looked away from him. "No! No, she won't!" She crossed her arms. "There's nothing you can do about it happening? It's going to happen." Anna looked at the floor in front of her and remained silent. After a while he said, "But it will be different from you -- she has a mother who understands." "I had my aunt!" "Yes, Elisabeth understood you." "Having a child before marriage is even worse than killing someone for many here." "Much worse! The purity of Jewish women, that's a myth. The weal and woe of the whole nation depends on it, if not mankind ... the Jews are the chosen people." "Do you believe that all Jewish women must remain pure?" "No." He shook his head. "Not at all. Only the one who will bear the Messiah must .... The priests just forgot at some point how to know who she is. That's why I want everyone to stay pure." She took his hand. "You helped me a lot then. That was nice. That was --- that was nice for Elisabeth too. -- Do you really come all this way just for Sophia?" He looked at her questioningly, "Only? What do you mean?"

Sophia had come to the bench with her sister and jumped onto Simeon's lap with a flourish.
He caught her with his arms, groaned briefly and then laughed out loud. Sophia leaned back and enjoyed for a moment the warm calm and security that radiated from Simeon.
Tears came to his eyes.
Maria pushed herself closer and pushed Sophia's leg away, "I want a turn too."
Sophia stood up and Mary jumped onto Simeon's lap in the same way she had.


"We have to keep going," Anna said, standing up and reaching for the girls' hands. The girls waved at their mother's hand. Then she picked up the handcart, smiled once more at Simeon and they walked towards the town. He looked after them for a long time.
A little later Sophia said to Anna, "He must always cry when he sees me. What is it, Mother?" "Ask him ..." Sophia nodded: "Mh mh."
