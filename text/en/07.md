# Chapter 7

Amon let out a long cry, jumped over a tree trunk and tried to catch up with his brother Gallum. He ran up a small hill, laughing and waving Amon's shoe. He climbed up a rock, stood wide-legged at the top and held his shoe out to Amon. Amon came up the hill out of breath and with a red head. He was fuming: "Give me back my shoe! This is not funny. You mountain monkey!" he shouted up to Gallum and began to climb up the rock. Gallum laughed and the moment Amon could just reach his leg, he jumped down from the rock and landed right in front of Nahor, who had followed them and was also laughing all over his face. "This is not funny!" shouted Amon to the two as he climbed back down. At the bottom he immediately tried to grab his shoe, but Gallum pulled it away, hid it behind his back and threw it to Nahor a moment later. He waggled the shoe in front of Amon's face as suddenly all three froze and looked down the hill. "Was that ...?" asked Nahor, and Amon said, "YES! That was a wolf." Nahor dropped the shoe and all three ran down the hill towards the flock and sure enough, a wolf had grabbed a sheep that had strayed a little from the flock. Four other wolves were waiting in a semi-circle behind it, baring their teeth and growling menacingly as the three came running.
Gallum reached the wolves first. Still running, he pulled a knife from his trousers and, without waiting, leapt in a great leap directly onto the back of the wolf that had bitten into the sheep. He slashed the knife with all his might right between the wolf's eyes, which howled and took Gallum with it as it jumped up. Gallum managed to stay on top of the wolf, pinning it to the ground, and slammed the knife into the wolf's head a second time, then again, and again. The other wolves attacked. Amon pulled his stone slingshot out of his pocket and hit the first running wolf in the head. The wolf howled loudly. He shot again and hit it again. The wolf staggered. Nahor had picked up a stone and hit one of the other wolves with it, running to one of the piles of stones he always made when they stopped somewhere. There he picked up more stones and threw them one after the other. Every stone hit. He and Amon chased the running wolves to the next hill. They watched the wolves until they disappeared in a bush.

"We need dogs!" said Amon. "We need to raise dogs, strong, fast and smart dogs." Nahor laughed, "Strong, fast and clever dogs that will obey us: Where will you find them? Dogs that wolves are afraid of?" Amon nodded, "Yes, there must be." "Then there's no need for us shepherds." "Yes we do, to train the dogs." Nahor looked at him in surprise, then nodded, "Mhmh. Maybe." He smiled. When they got back to Gallum, he was already skinning the wolf. The head lay cut off in the sand. He had cut the sheep's neck and was bleeding it dry. Nahor knelt down beside the sheep, put both hands at its side and began to pray. "I'll get the sheep together," Amon said and walked a little further down the hill. A loud whistle was heard and after a few moments Amon returned with the flock of sheep in his wake. He sat down next to Nahor and said, "With two dogs we could have a much bigger flock." Nahor nodded, "Yes, that would be possible. But where are there such dogs? And how do we train them to look after a flock of sheep?"
Gallum, meanwhile, had dismembered the wolf and laid the pieces in the grass. "I'll build a fire. Will you give me your stone, Amon?" Amon pulled a flint from his trouser pocket and threw it to Gallum. He caught it with his foot, balanced it and kicked it into the air so that he could catch it comfortably with his hand. He grinned broadly. Amon shook his head and turned to Nahor, "Let's skin and hang the sheep, we'll give that to the innkeeper when we get back." The nodded.
Half an hour later, all three were sitting around a large fire. Gallum arranged the burning branches and twigs with a stick and added new ones. "Soon we can eat. I'm looking forward to it." "I don't eat wolf meat. I'll stick to my dates," Amon said. Gallum rolled his eyes.
Nahor asked Amon, "Has the wrong hooves, doesn't it?"
"And is not a ruminant."
"Well, leave out the hooves and the stomach," Gallum growled between them, "would you let the good meat rot here?"
"There are plenty of animals that eat that, all that would be left of the wolf in two days would be bones," Amon looked sternly at Gallum, "That's not meat for humans."
He looked back indifferently, "It's pretty tough, okay, but you can manage it with a good hot ember. Since you were in Jerusalem last autumn, you've been really strange about some things."
Nahor put a hand on Gallum's shoulder, "It's our tradition in Judea that we only eat certain meats. It's just that not so many keep to it ... Not anymore. It used to be different."
Gallum: "We live today, not in the past. What actually happened in Jerusalem that you changed so much in your life, Amon?"
Nahor nodded, "Yes, I'd like to know that too. Was that the priest you met?"
Amon nodded, "Mhmh. Simeon."
Nahor looked at him, "So? You meet a priest and he tells you something and you believe it all? Amon, this is not you? You usually want to know everything in detail and you don't believe anything so easily."
"That's exactly why. He knew so much. It was so logical what he said. He could answer any question I ever had. Just like that. He would look up in the air for a moment and then give me answers that I understood immediately. We sat by a pond and talked for many hours."
"Why did he do that? A priest from Jerusalem? Our priest only says to me to come to the synagogue on Saturday when I ask him something."
"Simeon is different. He knows everything, I think, pretty much everything, and he came up to me in the middle of the street and wanted to talk to me."
Gallum looked at him bored, "Because you're special, huh? A shepherd from Bethlehem? Hey! ... Brother, stay on the ground."
Amon closed his eyes. None of the three spoke for a long time. Then Amon lifted his head and opened his eyes again, "The Messiah is coming! That's what he told me --- and that only the simple people will understand him --- those who live by their hearts, those who are connected to the earth --- and he is coming very soon, he said."
"And you didn't just believe that, you understood that? How can you understand such a thing?", Nahor asked him curiously.
Amon nodded. "We have lost everything here in Jude --- there are no more prophets --- it is no longer real what our priests say, they want to educate us and give us precepts, but they no longer understand the laws and the holy book --- they no longer hear the great voice of our past --- and people no longer love the laws Moses gave us, they have forgotten what they mean --- there are no more immaculate births --- and everywhere people are possessed by all kinds of demons, in every village you have people ..."
Nahor placed a hand gently on Amon's shoulder. Then he put both hands on his chest and closed his eyes. After a while he asked without opening his eyes, "Immaculate births? Why? Why is that important? What have we lost there?"
"The Messiah, when he comes, cannot be born into a body that is unclean. And his mother must also be born of a pure body."
Nahor nodded, "Yes. I understand that."
Gallum: "What is that, an immaculate birth?"
Amon: "It starts with the child being conceived pure."
Gallum scowled at him.
Amon continued, "That's what Simeon explained to me. He said it is the greatest joy you can imagine when a child crosses the great threshold into life at conception. For father and mother it is actually like when you meet your best friend again, after many years, so much has happened in the meantime, and he stands in front of you and you realise it is him! And the whole thing is 100 times stronger. But we don't experience it directly. Our body does, it is full of anticipation and gets ready when a person is to walk through that gate in it, when it is invited by a woman and a man. It is an overwhelming feeling that surges through the whole body, in the man for a few seconds, back and forth throughout the body, a feeling of freedom, and in the woman, Simeon said, it lasts much longer. And into this joy of our body a child enters into life, into this welcoming joy of the parents --- except --- except the parents enjoy it for themselves, and not together with the child. Then they rejoice, but leave the child, do not see it at all, do not welcome it at all. And that is not an immaculate welcome, because the joy is there for the child, as for the friend. So you don't receive a best friend from whom you have been separated for a long time, that only you rejoice yourself, but not at all with him and directed towards him." Nahor said in amazement, "Yes. That is not an unsullied welcome, I understand." Amon: "And the Messiah must not enter into life defiled." Nahor: "People have forgotten how to do that welcome. I once heard that in primeval times all births were pure, that it was normal, but I never really understood what was meant by that." Amon: "And now the Messiah is coming. He wants to bring back to us what we have lost, teach us what we have forgotten and remind us of everything we have forgotten ... especially who we ourselves are. We need the Messiah, we need him now, before we have lost everything and cannot get it back!"

Nahor and Gallum were silent. Gallum poked the ground with his stick and after a while said, "But first we need two dogs." Nahor and Amon looked at each other and started laughing. "Yes, you're right, Gallum! First we need two dogs ..." Gallum took a piece of wolf meat from the fire and offered it to Nahor. He took it with a patty of bread and bit into it. The sun was setting and they all looked out over the flock of sheep into the valley, which shone in wonderful colours under an evening red sky. Gallum put a hand on Amon's shoulder. He put a date in his mouth and began to chew.
