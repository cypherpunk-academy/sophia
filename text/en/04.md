# Chapter 4

Six years later.

It was quite warm for a winter morning. The sun was just coming up. Sophia opened the door of the house and came out with a water jug. She ran to the well, filled it and came back with it and ran through the house to the animal enclosures. First she filled the drinking bowls of the two badgers who had their place just to the left of the door to the garden. Then came the ducks and geese, the chickens on the right and her pig, which had the biggest enclosure because it was only here for a few weeks to prepare the ground for beds. It had already done a great job in the first few days, digging everything up once. Sophia smiled and went to the rabbit hutch.
The doe did not come hobbling up as usual, but remained lying under its roof. "Come on, fresh water," Sophia called to her, but the bunny didn't move, just looked over contentedly. Sophia filled her water bowl and pushed it as far as she could towards the doe. Then she saw a small head peeking out from under her front paw. Sophia opened the hutch and crawled inside. She stroked the doe over the head, pushed the water bowl close to her mouth and carefully ran her hand under her belly. She pulled out a tiny bunny whose eyes were still glued shut and who seemed to be searching for what was going on with its nose. Sophia held it gently against her chest in both hands and said softly, "Welcome." The bunny was not afraid, not even when Sophia put it back next to its mother. It oriented itself a little and then crawled back under its mother's belly into the straw where she was lying.
Sophia stood up, turned towards the house and called out, "Maria!!! Come! Quick! The new bunnies are here. A few moments later, Maria appeared very sleepily at the door to the garden and plodded to the rabbit hutch. She crawled in and took out a bunny, held it in front of her and looked at it. "Good," she said firmly, "they seem healthy." She gave it to Sophia and got the next one. She did so until the last one, which felt limp as she pulled it out. It had no body tension, didn't stir. Maria held it in front of her and looked at it. "There's something wrong with this one." She pressed it gently to her ear. "Ohhhh. I don't think it's alive." Sophia picked it up and smelled it. "But it doesn't seem to have been dead long." She crouched down on the floor and held the bunny close to her heart. She held her breath. For a long time. Suddenly she took a deep, slow breath. The bunny twitched imperceptibly and raised its head slightly. Sophia held it to her ear again and smiled. She held it out to Maria, who also took it to her ear. She laughed. "Yes! It's alive again!!!"
"Anna! Anna!" a woman shouted from the street in front of the house. So loud that Sophia and Maria could hear it well. There was something very frightening in the voice. Sophia and Maria looked at each other. They started running. Sophia in front. Their mother had already opened the door and Clara, Lidia's mother, was standing outside, her head red and her eyes puffy. Lidia was two years older than Sophia, they had played together a lot in the past, especially with the animals in the backyard. When Anna reached Clara, she sank to her knees and grabbed Anna's arm. "Anna! Please! Please! Lidia! Lidia. Lidia." Anna lowered her head and Clara whispered something in her ear. "FOR GOD'S Sake!" cried Anna. "NO! NO! It can't be. I'm coming with you! Wait ..." She turned to get something in the house. Sophia blocked her way, "What's wrong?" Anna looked at her and away and back again. "It's not for you!" "I want to come with you!" demanded Sophia. Anna pushed her aside and said as she passed, "No, it's not for you. You stay here!" She returned with a jacket and shoes and pulled Sophia into the house. "You wait here. I'll be back soon." Maria huddled behind her sister. Anna pulled the door shut and Sophia heard her say to Clara in the street, "Come! I'm going to talk to him. It can't be. It can't be. There's something very wrong."
Sophia grabbed the door handle, leaned her head against the door and counted backwards: "30, 29, 28, 27, 26 ..."
