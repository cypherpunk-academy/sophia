# Chapter 2

The old shepherd rose with difficulty from the bench in front of his house. He clutched his stomach and screwed up his face. "Gloomy again!" he snarled, shouting loudly. "Where are my boys?" He groaned. Nahor, his eldest son gently put his hand on his back. "We are all here already. --- Take your time. --- Do as you can, it is a long way down to the host in the village." Gallum, the youngest and by far the strongest, jumped with his back in front of him and crouched. "Jump up, father! Grab hold of me! Grip well. I'll carry you all the way down to the inn." Amon, the middle, gaunt one, weighed perhaps half as much as Gallum and always limped a little because he had a shoe on his right foot and none on his left. It was a Roman shoe he had found on the way to Jerusalem. It was his first, his brothers had none. He held out his hand to his father: "Come on, let's go. And I can speak for you at the innkeeper's, if you want. I'll tell him to take us!" The father wrapped his arms around Gallum's neck and grumbled, "You'll do nothing. I'll talk. You shut up until I say you talk." Amon nodded and repeated, "I'll be quiet until you say I'll talk." Gallum grabbed his father's legs, stood up with him and the four of them made their way down to the village of Bethlehem.

The fat innkeeper stood broad-legged in front of his house, looking grimly with his arms folded at the group coming down the hill towards his house. In front of him Gallum dismounted his father and he immediately walked up to the innkeeper, "Levi!" He held out his hand. The innkeeper gave him his. "Levi! We have known each other since we were little children. Long before you had the flock of sheep. I am sick, very sick. The rabbi says I will die in a month." The host acknowledged this with a barely audible "Mh mh". He looked at the father, "What do you want?" "Here, see my children. They are young and strong and clever and prudent. I can't take care of them anymore, but they've helped me with your flock since they were little and I've taught them everything I know." Amon: "And we chased the wolves away. They're wild this year." The father and the host both looked at him crossly. Father: "Be quiet!" And directed to the innkeeper, "I want you to take them for me. You want them to have my place?" "How???" the innkeeper roared, "all three of them???" They are so few that you need three of them to replace you. The father startled and shook his head. "No, no, ... yes. You've been doing this for a while. It's not like you need more money, just enough to cover my wife and two girls." The landlord shook his head. "NO! And I've already promised your place to my brother-in-law. Three are enough shepherds. Your children will have to find another flock." The three looked at their father in amazement. "He doesn't want us?" asked Amon? "He needs us," said Nahor. "The other two can't manage. The herd is too big." "And his brother-in-law ... he can't do it anyway." Gallum took a step forward, looked menacingly at Levi and shouted, "You don't want us?" He looked back grimly. Gallum leapt towards him and raised his fist. The landlord held his out to him. They looked each other straight in the face, their noses almost seeming to touch. They stood there, all holding their breath.
"WÖLFEEE!" someone called down the path from above. "WÖLFEEE! Quick! NAAAHHOOOR --- AAAMMONNN --- GAAALLLUUUMMM ---." He arrived at the innkeeper's house, panting. "Good ..., good to find you in a moment. You come quickly, the wolves are back, many, many, many -- five or six!" The innkeeper grabbed him by the collar and pulled him towards him. He shouted at him. "Do it yourselves! You are shepherds! Surely you can handle a few wolves in broad daylight?" The shepherd looked at him helplessly, "Without those three?" He shook his head wildly. The host gripped tighter and shouted, "WAAASSS?" The shepherd turned pale and continued to shake his head. The innkeeper pushed him off, so that he landed in the dust. He picked himself up and ran. The three ran off with him. Amon stopped after a few metres and shouted "HAAALLLTT!!!" He turned around and shouted to the innkeeper, "Are you hiring us?" The innkeeper cursed loudly and made a hand sign that probably meant he agreed. "All three of you?" shouted Amon. The innkeeper repeated the hand signal. Then they ran away.
The father sat down on the ground in the sand, breathing heavily. The innkeeper came up to him, slapped him on the shoulder and said, "Come inside, we'll have some wine until your boys come back. You really seem to be able to do something."
