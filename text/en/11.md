# Chapter 11

Anna entered the small temple in the village of Cana where Zechariah usually prepared his sacrifices. She knelt down and placed first her hands, then her forehead on the ground. When she straightened up again, Zachariah stood in front of her and smiled joyfully at her.
Anna: "Zach! How good to see you! --- I'm here to ask you something."
Zachariah nodded and led her to a bench at the side of the temple room.
"It's about Sophia. She is not well after seeing the stoning."
Zacharias pointed a finger at Anna.
"Me? I'm fine."
He looked at her questioningly.
She closed her eyes and felt inside, "No, I'm fine. I'm a little upset, but nothing compared to Sophia."
He looked at her slightly grimly and indicated that she should put her hand on her heart.
She closed her eyes again and after a while said, "I feel guilty for not taking better care of Sophia. I knew she would come after me, but I didn't pay enough attention. I even forgot about it then. She really shouldn't have seen the stoning."
Zachariah shook his head and pointed to a picture of the sun on the altar.
"You mean God wanted it that way? You mean this is part of their destiny?"
He nodded a few times.
"But she is distraught. She doesn't eat properly anymore, lies in bed all day, doesn't feed her animals."
He closed his eyes. After a while he opened them and tried to symbolise the earth or the community of people by spreading his arms out in a circle. And then crossing this sign with one arm. But Anna did not understand. He repeated it twice more, without success, and then thought. Suddenly he stood up and told Anna to wait with his hand. Then he ran out of the temple. After long minutes he came back and had a man about his age by the hand. He pointed to Anna and then to the man.
"Joseph," he said, holding out his hand to Anna. "Josef, the carpenter."
Anna beamed, "How nice to meet you for once, Josef! My daughters love your rocking horse, Sophia and ..."
Joseph interrupted, "Maria. I know Zacharias has told me a lot about you. It's because of you that the great Simeon always comes up here from Jerusalem, isn't it?"
Anna nodded.
Joseph sat down with her on the bench and smiled warmly at her. Anna had to laugh a little because of the pleasant atmosphere that emanated from him and wafted over to her.
He asked, "Why did Zach come for me? What can I do to help?"
Anna: "I don't know. Sophia saw a stoning, by one of her friends, and now she's at home lying on the bed and doesn't really want to go back to life."
Joseph listened but said nothing.
Anna: "She stopped feeding her animals. She had mingled with the spectators in boy's clothes, then stood in the front row and just before the first stone flew, wanted to throw herself in front of Lidia. I just managed to intercept her."
Joseph put a hand over his heart and closed his eyes. After a long while he asked Anna, "Did she scream as loud as she could?".
"Phew, and how. I can still hear the first scream now. And then she screamed again and again all the way home."
"Good!" said Joseph, "did she speak anything on the way?"
Anna thought, "Yes, she asked, Why Lidia? That can't be."
Joseph: "How is Mary?"
"She hasn't seen any of it. She is doing well. She feeds the animals and tries to do Sophia good where she can."
"Then all is well, I think. Can you wait a few more days and watch what happens?"
"No. I don't want to. I've already waited."
Joseph thought. "I can come and talk to her too."
"Would you?"
Joseph nodded, "I'd love to. I would be honoured. The girl Simeon comes from Jerusalem for."
Anna: "Thank you. When can you come?"
Joseph looked at her, "Now?"
Anna laughed and hugged him.
