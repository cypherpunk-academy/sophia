# Chapter 21

One morning a few weeks later, black clouds of smoke were seen in the sky over Nazareth from the courtyard. This was the sign for all the inhabitants to come together and hear what a Roman crier had to say. Sophia stretched her feet a little outside the temple house. It was very cold, winter was just around the corner, and her steps had become heavier. Her belly seemed huge. Sometimes the child moved noticeably and every time she did, shivers of joy ran down her spine. At those moments she felt light and free, as if her heart was reaching far out of her body. She sometimes wondered how that was possible.
Joseph and Klophas stood in the courtyard looking towards the smoke.
Klophas: "That's not a farm burning, that's the crier fire." He looked over at Josef, who was looking anxiously at the ground.
Joseph: "Yes, let's go, both of us. They want to see you too."
They both said goodbye to Sophia and admonished her not to go too far from the cottage. She assured them not to, it would be too cold to stay outside for long anyway.
In the village they found a large crowd in the square where Lidia had been stoned to death earlier that year. There was a small platform in the middle, but it was still empty. Next to it they could see tables and many clay tablets on which the names and the taxes paid were to be carved. Behind them stood a row of Roman soldiers. All wearing identical armour, all with the same spear in front of them and the same deadpan expression on their faces. Joseph looked at them, stopped and grabbed his stomach. Klophas patted him on the back. Now you must be strong. At that moment the priest appeared behind them with a dark face, but in a good mood.
The priest stood up in front of them: "Well, are you coming to be counted?"
Joseph: "We'll listen to what they say first."
Priest: "I can help you there. I have been to Jerusalem and heard this twice: everyone must be counted, with his full name and pay taxes according to his status. But he can only do that where he comes from. And if one evades that, then that threatens there!" He pointed to the end of the square where there was a wooden cross about three metres high.
Joseph and Klophas had not seen the cross before. A shock ran through both of them. They had heard of crosses, but had not seen one. In Rome, foreign criminals were tied or nailed to them and displayed until they died. Romans themselves were not put on crosses, no matter what they had done. Joseph looked at the priest. At that moment Anna walked past with Mary. They looked over briefly, nodded happily and walked on without reacting in any particular way. The priest looked questioningly into Joseph's eyes. He did not let on.
A small man in a colourful, seemingly valuable uniform climbed the platform. At the signal of a soldier, a soldier began to drum loudly. Everything became quiet.
The man called out: "Jews! You are gathered here today to seal your bond with Rome. You are part of the great Roman Empire, the greatest and most powerful empire that has ever existed. Rome has taken care of you, given you a good ruler after you had inferior ones before, built roads, waterways and many great buildings. Your lives have become richer, you find goods from many countries in your markets, many of you have been to the capital of the Empire. Even a man from this city. Now it is time for you to firstly take a new step and secondly give something back. The new step is to confess your name before Rome as part of the Roman Empire. And the giving back is the tax that is no longer to be paid by each city or each family, but by each individual man. Every man of twenty years and over shall pay an annual tax to Rome from this day forth. How much you will learn from the scribes. You have one moon to raise the money. Only then can your name be put on one of the tablets that will be registered in Jerusalem. If your name is not on it and the moon is over, Rome has no patience with you. Such a great empire cannot be ruled with patience. If a moon has passed and your name is not on such a tablet, then this awaits you:' He turned theatrically and pointed with both arms at the wooden cross. A murmur went through the crowd. "It will take you one to three days to die from this. We will put up as many wooden crosses as are needed. You will get nothing to drink during this time but vinegar, no water." He paused and looked at the crowd around him. Then he continued, "One more thing: we have collected old family lists in Jerusalem. With these we will match the names on the tablets. Whoever is missing will be sought by bounty. Adventurers will look for these men, neighbours in dispute will denounce, priests will help us." The crowd grew more restless. The man looked around the whole round again. Then he continued, "And one more thing! You can only enter your name where your family is from. Any entry elsewhere is invalid and will be cancelled. Even if you have paid your money. That is all!"
The man looked round once more, then left the platform and walked towards a Roman chariot drawn by two horses. A soldier boarded it with him and they drove off.
Anna came up behind Joseph and Clophas. She seemed very excited.
Anna: "I just overheard a shepherd boy come up to the priest and say that he had seen Sophia on the farm and that she was heavily pregnant. He said he recognised her for sure. The priest immediately jumped up, told some people in the crowd and then ran away!!!"
Joseph: "For God's sake! NO!" He looked around.
Clophas: "We have to get there. We have to take her away."
Mary came out from behind Anna: "We must warn her. She must know!"
Joseph shouted, "GO! We'll go together. It's all the same now. It's clear now anyway."
He took a step and kicked a hole in the ground with his left foot and crumpled. He cried out loudly. Anna bent down to his foot.
Anna: "Oh, no!" She held his foot and Josef sat down, but got right back up.
Josef: "It's alright. We have to get going. We can't be late." Anna supported Josef and he limped as best he could, but after a few steps it was clear that he was too slow.
Josef said in a strong voice, "Klophas!"
Klophas stood in front of him, "Yes!"
Joseph: "You are a man now! You have the task, with all your skill and strength, to bring Sophia to safety from the priest! I have no further instructions. You have to figure out how to do it. You have my full support. Do you understand?"
Klophas: "Clearly and distinctly! And Maria is a woman now and helps me with that!"
Anna raised her index finger.
Klophas gently pushed it away and looked Anna in the eye, "I'm a man and I'm saying this now because I need Maria to do my job!"
Mary laughed at him and they both ran off.
Joseph and Anna both shook their heads.
Anna: "We still have to go and try to get to the farm as soon as possible."
Just before they were out of town, Klophas and Maria saw the group around the priest, about ten to twelve men standing in the street. Klophas pulled Maria into a side street, "Come on here. We have to cross the meadows. I know some shortcuts."
They ran as fast as they could, over fields and hills until they came to the path where the group would probably soon pass. Klophas gestured to Maria that he didn't want to go that way, but through the olive grove and the copse behind the farm. He wanted to come to the farm from behind and found the way at the first attempt. Suddenly, just before the farm, he was startled, stopped and stopped Maria.
Klophas: "Oh, God! There's someone in the courtyard square. I can't see it clearly. --- It's soldiers! --- Two. They're looking. --- What do we do?" He squeezed his eyes shut and after a few moments opened them and said, "I know! You go slowly and directly behind the temple cottage and try to warn Sophia. If you can escape with her, go to the cave. I'll pick you up there then."
Maria: "What are you doing?"
Klophas: "I'm distracting the soldiers."
Maria: "Ohhhh."
Clophas: "Don't worry, Maria, I can do it. I'm good at that sort of thing."
He stroked her arm, "See you in a minute!" Then he walked towards the back of the carpenter's shop.
Maria slowly crept closer to the temple cottage.
Klophas walked past the carpentry shop on the right and into the courtyard square, where one of the two soldiers was looking around. At the edge of the square stood two white, large horses. The other soldier was just coming out of the apartment building.
Klophas looked in amazement, a little theatrically, at the soldiers. Then he shouted in a loud voice, "What are you doing here? This is not your farm!"
The two soldiers turned towards him and Klophas walked up to the one and stood in front of him. They were both very tall, with powerful statures, and had nicer, heavier armour than the other two he had met here a few weeks ago. Their swords were ornate on the hilts. He was irritated and in a strange way attracted by the elegance with which they both stood before him. But he tried not to let it show.
Klophas: "What do you want? This is Joseph's carpentry workshop. He's on his way here."
The soldier in front of him exhaled in relief, "This is Josef's carpentry shop! That's what we were looking for." He eyed Klophas. "And then you must be Klophas, hmm?"
"Brave boy," said the other soldier behind him.
The soldier nodded. "Yes. From what we've heard."
Klophas: "How do you know my name? Why are you here?"
Soldier: "We are looking for Sophia!"
Klophas was startled. He could not hide it.
Soldier: "We mean her no harm. On the contrary. I am her ..."
The other soldier shouted loudly, "Down there, there's a group coming! With a priest in front."
Soldier: "Then we are just in time."
Clophas looked at them in confusion, then at the group coming up the driveway armed with sticks. He asked the soldiers, "Who are you?"
Soldier: "You'll find out in a minute! Right now we have to sort this out first."
Both soldiers ran with long strides towards the stairway and stood in the way of the group.
The priest approached them, "Greetings, soldiers! Please clear the way, we have to do our duty here as Jews and I as a priest. And you do not interfere. There is no danger for Romans. This is an internal Jewish matter."
Both soldiers folded their arms and looked at the priest.
Priest: "You will not commit a wrong here that twelve men have seen? Let us through! It is a serious situation."
The soldier who had been talking to Klophas looked intently at the priest: "You want Sophia?"
Priest: "We want Sophia!"
Soldier: "You can't have her! Get out of here!" Both soldiers loosened their arms.
The priest blushed: "We are twelve men! And we'll take them if necessary, we have to, by our laws!"
The group moved, some holding their sticks higher. The man next to the priest grabbed his and was about to lift it upwards when the other soldier's sword went past his ear like a bolt of lightning and the soldier pressed it against his head.
Soldier: "Let us all be prudent! I would be fine with twelve of you on my own. You all haven't fought real Roman soldiers yet. Otherwise you wouldn't have said that." The other soldier drew his sword upwards and cut off a lock of the man's hair. Then he put his sword away again.
The priest was paralysed with shock. After a few moments he asked in a trembling voice, "Why are you protecting Sophia?"
Soldier: "You don't recognise me?" He took off his helmet.
The priest went down on his knees, "Oh, no, oh, no! Forgive me!"
Soldier: "Now get out of here as fast as you can. And if any of you so much as look askance at my daughter, I will kill him! And that's for the rest of your lives."
The priest and the group turned and ran away from the courtyard.
Klophas stood with his mouth open, "Daughter?" He swallowed. "You are Joachim?"
The soldier laughed, "Yes, that's me! And this is Leonidas, my friend from Rome." They both held out their hands to Klophas and he took them in turn, still a little shy but mighty proud. Leonidas pulled him close, pressed his face to his and said, "Strong entrance just now, with two Roman soldiers you didn't know."
Joachim: "Now tell us where she is. We haven't found her."
Clophas: "They are surely by now in the cave where we were to meet. Come ..."
At that moment Maria and Sophia came out of the door of the temple cottage. Maria jumped towards her father and fell around his neck. He picked her up and turned with her. Then he put her down and took Sophia in his arms. She was beaming.
Joachim: "My Sophia! That's you! You are upsetting the whole world. You already did that when you weren't even born. I am so happy to be with you. I just got back from Rome a fortnight ago. Simeon told me how things were and then I took a few days off to look after things here. Where is your mother?"
Clophas: "She will be here soon."

With pain, Joseph walked step by step, Anna supporting him, and at last they turned into the valley. They looked up at the farm and saw an excited group running away from the farm.
Joseph fell to his knees: "Oh, no! They were already there and they are leaving again. They have Sophia." Anna bent down to him and hugged him. After a while she pulled him up, "Come on, maybe there's something else we can do." They walked slowly on, towards the group.
Anna: "I don't see Sophia. And they're moving pretty fast, she can't be going that fast."
Joseph looked up. He straightened up, recognising the priest coming towards them with quick steps. Joseph started to say something, but the priest paid no attention to him and walked past without a word. So did the rest of the group.
Anna and Josef looked after them and wondered.
In the courtyard square, Anna fell around Joachim's neck. She laughed at him, grabbed him by the shoulders and tried to dance with him in a circle.
Joachim: "I will now put a sign on your house in Nazareth that the priest and none of his people are allowed to come near. With a Roman seal!"
Mary snuggled up to him from behind, "Yessss! Please do that!"
Joachim: "You've just been away for a while and already you're doing things like this!"
Anna: "Five years, barely." She shook her head.
Joachim: "At least I can make sure you are safe now. A lot has changed in those five years."
Anna: "I see you travel with horses now. And I see a little more gold on you too."
Joachim: "A lot has happened. We will find time, then I will tell you everything."
Joachim became thoughtful. "I cannot, however, ensure the safety of all of us." He looked at Joseph. I am powerless against Herod. He is a friend of Emperor Augustus. And he no longer cares if I have a Jewish family as a Roman soldier, that's fine by now. But he is looking at the possible heirs to the throne, Joseph, and he knows you are here. I learned that in Jerusalem. You have to go to Bethlehem! Otherwise it will be very difficult."
Joseph nodded.
Sophia: "I'll go with him!"
Anna: "NO! You're going to have your baby soon. You can't go to Bethlehem in the middle of winter now! I won't allow it."
Joachim: "I allow it. She is right. Even if it looks crazy. But it's the only chance. She cannot stay in Nazareth, even if the priest does not harm her. It is well known in Jerusalem that a 14-year-old Sophia, who is expecting a child, is being hidden here. There is the whole Jewish culture in the way, which does not allow that. I can't take her either. I can't do anything about that. The Romans don't interfere with Jewish culture. If we want to kill ourselves, they'll let us. The best thing is for her to travel with Joseph, in a foreign country where no one knows her, it looks like a marriage. And right now there are a lot of strangers on the road."
Anna looked at him aghast, "You want your daughter to be alone ..."
Sophia interrupted, "Mother! It is my way. I know it is. It looks impossible, but it is my way. I must go to Bethlehem with Joseph."
Joachim held out a leather sack of Roman coins to Joseph, "Simeon told me that you always spend all the money you take in, you don't put anything back. You might be able to use this now."
Joseph took it, "Thank you! We'll take the donkey and the ox with us. After all, I have to pay my taxes too."
Joachim slapped Josef on the shoulder, "I have a good feeling, but you must be away from here before sunrise tomorrow. There is an examiner who looks after the heirs to the throne, he is on his way. And maybe here tomorrow. If he sees you here, then it could get complicated. He might take you with him."
Joseph and Sophia looked at each other and nodded.
Klophas: "I have another idea! What if Sophia and Mary swapped names? You Romans could make it official, couldn't you? Then when someone asks for 14-year-old Sophia who is expecting a child, we show them Maria and say it's Sophia. Then Sophia is a 12-year-old who is not expecting a child. And when Sophia is asked on the way, she is called Maria and no one asks any more questions."
Leonidas: "That boy is great!" He pointed at him and looked at Joachim. "You must take him with you to Jerusalem. With a Roman education he will be able to achieve much."
Joachim thought for a moment and then put his hand on Klophas' shoulder, "Well? Ready for an adventure? Here you have an empty courtyard. In Jerusalem I'll show you how to find your own way. Closer to the king, closer to the gallows, closer to freedom. So, what is your answer?"
Klophas smiled at him incredulously, then nodded significantly, "Yes. At least once for four years."
Joachim: - "Good!" He looked round. "Then we're through. We're already behind schedule. Let's get going, Leonidas. I'll take Klophas on horseback."
Leonidas: "No, me!"
Klophas grinned broadly.
Joachim hugged Anna and Maria together, "In a few weeks you'll get my seal over your door, then no one will harm you. And I'll see how things are in Jerusalem, maybe I can bring you to me." He went to Sophia: "I don't have to tell you anything. You will find your way. You always have. We will meet again soon." He looked at Joseph for a long time, "A king for my Sophia, that fits." He laughed. Joseph put a hand on his shoulder, "Thank you for everything!"
Leonidas gestured to Clophas to come to him. On the horse, he took the reins around Klophas and said, "I will show you Rome! You will be amazed ..." They rode off.
Anna went up to Joseph and put her arm around him: "We'll stay here until morning. Do you have any ointment for your foot?"
Josef nodded, "A very good one. It will do."
